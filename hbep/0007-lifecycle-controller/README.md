# HBEP-7: Lifecycle controller

## Summary

The lifecycle controller keeps each ModelDeployment's desired state true: the requested number of serving instances, on the allowed providers, until the deployment's end. It provisions Allocations with hedging and over-allocation, and the hedge winner is the first Allocation whose Instance serves. It qualifies each Instance, and verifies and warms it with real-shape requests before it takes traffic. It checks health and restarts malfunctions, plans make-before-break handovers from lease ends, and never releases a serving instance before its replacement serves while the deployment keeps its desired capacity. An Allocation left with no Instance follows its retention policy, keep by default, and an operator can repurpose it to another ModelDeployment. Inside each Allocation, the allocation agent starts, restarts and stops the Instance on the controller's word. Terms follow HBEP-3.

For alpha: one Instance per Allocation, one worker per Instance, no hedging across providers at once, k = 0 with every Instance qualified, and no repurposing across models. Where a provider's per-user limits leave no room for a replacement beside its predecessor, as under Helios's QOS `now`, a deployment hands over with a planned gap and still cuts no request (no-overlap mode).

## Motivation

This HBEP serves stage 2 of the user story (HBEP-2), getting resources on HPC, and stage 3, keeping instances running through failures, restarts, time limits and handovers. Releases also serve stage 7.

Problems:
- Follow-ons pinned to the same node pended for days. Dependencies counted from the parent's start, not its end. Cancelling a parent started its pending follow-on at once.
- A slot freed before the replacement ran went to another user within seconds, and the replacement then waited days.
- One handover design cancelled the predecessor as soon as the follow-on answered, and gave back a large share of every lease.
- Users cannot extend time limits, and centers may not act at weekends. Serving jobs reached their end with no cover.
- Start estimates swung by hours, and actual starts often came earlier.
- An unwarmed follow-on tripped a client's load guard on its first requests. JIT compiles on unseen shapes stalled or killed engines, and warming up busy workers evicts live requests.
- Failing servers were waited out for hours instead of restarted.
- A multi-node rank 0 exited cleanly after an engine time-out while the step waited on the other node, so nothing restarted.
- A follow-on aborted itself seconds after start, on a failed status query of its ended predecessor. Stacks were down for over an hour unnoticed.
- Restart helpers written for one topology stopped their own workers on another. A router hung in graceful shutdown.
- Identity checks hashed the serve scripts, so a live script could not be edited, and an older job's restart picked up a newer script.
- Conversions of idle stacks between models were done by hand with per-job files, and job names stopped matching what they served.
- Editing a pending job's resources left it unschedulable for hours.
- A deliberate restart that was not recorded beforehand was counted as a crash.
- An interrupted multi-cluster release had already cancelled jobs on two clusters before it was reported as refused.

### Goals

- A declared deployment stays served until its end, with no hand-run handovers, with planned gaps in no-overlap mode (owner, #145).
- Every instance passes verification and a real-shape warm-up before it takes traffic.
- Over-allocation keeps only Instances that pass qualification, and the best of those.
- While a deployment keeps its desired capacity, no serving instance is released before its replacement serves.
- Failing instances restart promptly, in the topology their spec declares.
- A controller restart or an interrupted operation is safe: actions are idempotent, and partial failures are reported item by item.
- Repurposing an Allocation to another ModelDeployment is one declared operation.
- An Allocation with no Instance is kept or released as its retention policy says, not by hand.

### Non-goals

- Raising priority at a center. The framework does not assume centers push our jobs; its lever is hedging.
- Deciding what to serve, or which Allocations to repurpose. Users and agents set desired state and ask for repurposes (HBEP-10).
- Calling providers. Each Provider's own controller does that (HBEP-4).
- Routing (HBEP-5) and paths (HBEP-6).
- Choosing serving settings. Those are the serving spec (HBEP-3) and platform profiles (HBEP-4).

## Proposal

### User stories

- A researcher declares `example-27b` with two instances on Helios or Athena until a given date. Helios' GH200 partition and Athena's A100 partition are two accelerator classes, so the deployment has two current IDs, one per class (HBEP-3). The controller submits early to both and keeps the first Allocation whose Instance serves. That Instance takes the current ID of its Allocation's class. The controller cancels the pending loser, and the front routes to the Instance under `example-27b`. Before that lease's drain starts, a replacement already serves. Hedging across providers at once comes after alpha.
- In alpha, the same researcher allows only an Athena provider and sets a lease limit of four hours, to try a handover in an afternoon. The controller submits one Allocation. It submits the replacement at the time open question 2 plans from the queue waits observed on that Provider, or when the first Allocation starts while none has been observed. The replacement's Instance is verified and warmed, and serves beside the first. At the first lease's drain start, the first Instance drains, and no request is cut.
- On Helios, the researcher's Provider runs under QOS `now`, which allows one GPU per user and at most 12 h; the partition's own QOS allows 2,000 submitted jobs per user (HBEP-4, QOS limits). The controller submits one Allocation with a 12 h limit. A replacement can be submitted but cannot run beside it, so the deployment runs in no-overlap mode. At its planned time the replacement is submitted and pends. At the drain start the Instance drains, no request in flight is cut, and the front answers new requests with 503 `no_ready_target`. Once the first job has ended and freed its GPU, the replacement starts, and the front routes again when its Instance is ready. The gap is measured.
- A researcher needs four instances of `example-27b`. The controller obtains six Allocations on two Providers, one extra on each (k = 1 per provider), and qualifies each Instance with an identity check and a short benchmark. One node fails outright, and its Allocation is released. The controller keeps the best four; the one that passed but ranked fifth is kept as warm capacity, as its retention policy says. Over-allocation with k above 0 comes after alpha.
- An instance stalls with requests in flight. The controller records the restart as an event, then restarts the instance within minutes.
- An agent's work on `example-8b` ends while its Allocations have hours left, and `example-27b` needs the same GPU type. The agent repurposes the Allocations. The controller drains each `example-8b` Instance, starts an `example-27b` Instance in the same Allocation, then verifies and warms it, and the front routes to it. No new job is queued. Repurposing across models comes after alpha.
- An experiment must finish by a deadline. Its retention policy keeps every Allocation until then. Allocations whose model is done wait as warm capacity, or are repurposed, instead of going back to the queue.
- The controller restarts in the middle of a handover. It re-reads desired state and lists its allocations by label, and nothing is submitted twice.

### Notes, constraints and caveats

- These are decided:
  - Hedging, as HBEP-3 defines it: submit early, on several providers if needed; keep the first Allocation whose Instance serves; cancel the pending losers (open question 1; owner, 29 Oct 2026).
  - Over-allocation with qualification, as HBEP-3 defines them: obtain N+k Allocations, qualify each Instance with an identity check plus a short benchmark against what the platform profile says to expect, and keep the best N. The rest are released, or kept as warm capacity, per the retention policy. The gate also catches broken nodes, not only slow ones. Alpha runs k = 0 and qualifies every Instance (open question 12). Beta's mechanism is Design details, Over-allocation and qualification (beta).
  - Retention policy: keep an Allocation until a date, a grant's end or a deadline; or release it when idle. Keep is the default (open question 13).
  - Repurposing: an operator may move an Allocation to another ModelDeployment when a model's work finishes. Conversions between models are this operation.
  - Replacement before release, as HBEP-3 scopes it: while a deployment keeps its desired capacity, never release a serving instance before its replacement serves. The cases are listed under Release in Design details.
  - A handover replaces the Instance with a new one, with its own metadata, in the same accelerator class. The deployment ID stays. A replacement placed in another class is a new Instance with that class's current ID (HBEP-3).
  - On batch providers, continuity comes through follow-ons: independent submissions, with no dependencies or node pins (open question 3).
  - Hedging across providers is our own controller, built on the Kubernetes API (HBEP-11).
  - "Gap on Helios, handover on Athena" (owner, 1 Nov 2026, [issue #145, comment 6557452958](https://github.com/groundnuty/plgrid-llmlab-hpc-burst/issues/145#issuecomment-6557452958)). Helios serves under QOS `now`: one 1-GPU Instance of up to 12 h, with a planned gap at each job end (no-overlap mode, Handover). The no-cut handover is shown on Athena A100 (Graduation criteria). The framework may use QOS `now` freely, since those are the owner's resources and responsibility; during development, test runs use it sparingly, because the owner's other projects share the login. No request for a better QOS is made to Cyfronet. Since 11 Nov 2026 (owner, "Athena alone"), alpha's gap is shown on Athena `plgrid-now`, and Helios's in beta.
- **Prior art for over-allocation.** Farley et al. call the strategy placement gaming: launch extra instances up front and keep the strongest ("More for your money: exploiting performance heterogeneity in public clouds", ACM SoCC 2012). Ou et al. take a keep-good, discard-bad approach to EC2 instance heterogeneity ("Is the same instance type created equal? Exploiting heterogeneity of public clouds", IEEE Transactions on Cloud Computing, 2013).
- An Allocation outlives its Instances (HBEP-3). Ending an Instance and releasing its Allocation are separate steps: the Instance ends, and the Allocation then follows its retention policy.
- The lifecycle controller acts on providers only through Allocation objects, which each Provider's controller reconciles with the core operations (HBEP-4). Submission rules, per-user job limits, time-limit effects on queueing and time zones are profile data. Timestamps reach the controller in UTC: Slurm prints local time (Europe/Warsaw) on Helios and Athena and UTC on Sano (spike S5, PR #19), and the provider plugin converts it (HBEP-4).
- An allocation's account cannot change once it runs, so the account is fixed at submission. Each provider has one account (HBEP-3), so picking the account is picking the provider.
- The drain window is the profile's `drainWindow` (HBEP-4), which HBEP-5 sets, or shorter for a ModelDeployment that sets `routing.maxRequestDuration` (Handover). The controller plans so that a replacement serves before the predecessor's drain starts; the drain rule under Handover says what happens when it does not.
- Nothing of the Instance runs on a login node (HBEP-4, open question 14), and a job step launched from a login node dies with the `srun` that started it (L-3.5). HBEP-6's host-dialed relays are the one exception: `srun` steps that carry bytes and nothing else, so their dying ends only a connection, never the Instance. So the Instance's processes are started inside the Allocation, by the allocation agent, which the Allocation's job runs (Design details).

### Risks and mitigations

- **The node is lost.** Releasing before the replacement serves gives the slot to another user. Mitigation: the decided rule, checked by a test. A retention policy can also keep an Allocation after its Instance ends.
- **Cancelling a loser starts its dependents.** Mitigation: no provider-side dependencies (open question 3).
- **A restart submits twice.** Every submission carries a tag in a field the provider stores, and the provider finds its jobs by that tag, never by scanning names. The tag supports recovery but does not enforce uniqueness: a provider can accept two submissions with one tag, and a lost submission reply leaves the outcome unknown until the provider lists by tag. Mitigation, from spike S4 (PR #32): in hold mode every job is submitted held, and only the recorded job's hold is ever lifted, so a duplicate stays held and is cancelled. In fallback mode only a job found by tag resolves an unknown outcome (open question 11).
- **The scheduler requeues a job.** Slurm can requeue a batch job and run its script again under the same job ID, and requeue is on by default on Helios, Athena and Sano (spike S5, PR #19). That can leave stale registrations, verification results or replacement work from the first execution behind. Each platform profile records its requeue behaviour (HBEP-4). HBEP-3 decides identity across executions (open question 13, from spike S4): submit every Allocation with `--no-requeue`, treat a requeue that still happens as a failure of its Instance, never lift a requeued job's hold again (except the known risk in HBEP-4), and replace it with a new Allocation. The failed Allocation is deleted, and its finalizer cancels the held job.
- **An ended job is read as missing.** Helios and Athena drop a finished job from `squeue` about 10 s after it ends (spike S5, PR #19). A controller that reads a missing job as never submitted would submit it again. Mitigation: the provider plugin reports an ended lease as existing and terminal, from accounting (HBEP-4), and nothing is resubmitted only because a job is missing from the queue.
- **A job without GPUs on a GPU-only platform.** Athena is meant for GPU jobs only, and its documentation warns that workloads without GPUs can lead to account suspension (spike S5, PR #19). Mitigation: every Allocation requests at least one GPU on a GPU partition, and the Slurm plugin refuses any other (HBEP-4). Whether a GPU Allocation kept idle as warm capacity counts is Cyfronet's to say; beta doesn't wait for it, and Athena's `maxIdle` of 30 min bounds the idle time meanwhile (open question 13).
- **Overlap costs grant hours.** A replacement submitted too early, on a short queue, runs beside its predecessor for most of the lease. Mitigation: the replacement is planned from observed queue waits (open question 2, owner decision of 30 Oct 2026), so on a short queue the overlap is only the planning margin, except while the Provider's kept waits hold a long early wait or a pend behind the user's cap (Handover, No-overlap mode, the cost); overlap capacity serves traffic (open question 4), and the cost is shown per deployment (Operational readiness 6).
- **Warm-up misleads.** A warm-up on short prompts leaves long-context shapes cold. Mitigation: warm-up shapes come from the deployment spec, and warm-up runs on the new instance before admission, never on live workers.
- **Warm-up never settles on noise.** With one request per shape, a request under 300 ms moved by more than 10% between rounds on the real run (issue #94), and a reasoning model's tool call varied in length. Mitigation: only fixed-length shapes are judged, with an absolute floor of 100 ms under the 10% (Instance start).
- **JIT caches that ignore their variables.** Under `--no-home` Apptainer v1.5.4 mounts and stages no home at all (`internal/pkg/runtime/engine/apptainer/container_linux.go` lines 2053–2056), so `$HOME` names a path the read-only image lacks. A cache that ignores its variables, as FlashInfer's and the CUDA compute cache did on the real run (issue #94), then fails to write, which can fail the engine's start. Mitigation: `FLASHINFER_WORKSPACE_BASE` and `CUDA_CACHE_PATH` also point at the cache directory, and such a failure fails the start like any other, with the engine's log in the report (The allocation agent, What it runs).
- **Verification checks a proxy.** The engine's fingerprint differs per site and is sometimes missing. Mitigation: verification compares the settings the Instance reports with the declared ones, and rehashes the checkpoint files (HBEP-3, HBEP-9). It never recomputes the deployment ID from reported settings.
- **Verification passes because it read nothing.** An engine that reports no settings would contradict none. Mitigation: the engine version, the image digest and the context length must be reported and match; any other declared setting that is not reported is recorded as not reported, never as matching (Design details).
- **A contradicted spec loops.** An engine that resolves a declared setting differently does so on every start. Mitigation: a settings contradiction stops provisioning in that accelerator class until the spec changes, with a condition and an event; it is never restarted. A workload shape that does not fit the engine's context limit stops the class too, but only until the shapes fit, so a workload edit lifts it (Instance start).
- **A reasoning model spends its budget thinking.** A check that asks for a short answer can get only reasoning back. Mitigation: with a reasoning parser declared, the tool-call smoke test and the behaviour check each ask for 4,096 more tokens, judge only the final answer, and report a reply with no final answer as `ReasoningBudgetExhausted`, never as a wrong answer (Instance start). The fixed 4,096 is a known limit of alpha. Greedy decoding can make a reasoning model loop until its budget runs out, so its checks sample with its own `generation_config.json` values and a fixed seed (The allocation agent, Checks).
- **A stale gauge reads as idle.** A failed `/metrics` read that left a zero in place would end a draining Instance early, or start the idle count. Mitigation: a gauge left out is unknown, never zero, and the controller takes no gauge-based action unless the gauges are fresh (Health and restarts).
- **Qualification ranks on noise.** A short benchmark can rank nodes by chance, for example while a shared filesystem is busy. Mitigation: each Instance is judged against the profile's expected performance, not only against the others. Alpha ranks nothing and gates on no throughput (open question 12). In beta a serving peer is outranked only by more than 10%, and a miss of the gate is measured twice (Over-allocation and qualification (beta)).
- **A wrong reference releases good nodes.** A reference measured another way, or before a site change, fails every Instance it judges, and each failure releases an Allocation. Mitigation: the reference is keyed on the deployment ID, the capacity flags and the shape, and measured by the gate's own method; the tolerance is wide; a miss is measured twice; an entry stops gating 90 days after its date; and an entry that two Allocations in a row fail is suspended for the deployment, short of `RestartLoop` (Over-allocation and qualification (beta); HBEP-4, Platform profile schema).
- **An extra blocks its own handover.** An extra created beside a pending primary would take the slot that primary reserves once it runs. Mitigation: an extra needs a slot beyond the reservations its deployment's primaries will hold (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), Its checks). In host-dialed mode that leaves none for a deployment that hands over.
- **Warm capacity is forgotten.** Kept Allocations spend grant hours with nothing on them. Mitigation: every retention policy ends at a date, a grant's end, a deadline or an idle limit; the profile's `maxIdle` caps idle time where a platform needs it; and the inventory lists kept Allocations (HBEP-10).
- **A repurpose inherits the old model's state.** Files staged for the old model, or another engine, are left in the Allocation. Mitigation: the new Instance passes the same verification as any other, against its own deployment's identity record (HBEP-9).
- **Restart loops.** Mitigation: a bounded number of attempts per rung, with backoff, then a new allocation, then a degraded deployment and an event (HBEP-8).
- **A started hedge loser costs grant hours.** Kept under `Keep`, an idle loser holds its GPU until the deployment's end or its hard end. Mitigation: a second member is submitted only when the first is predicted late, or has pended past its expected start (Hedging), never while the first is expected on time, so two members start together only when the first beats its own prediction. The loser is warm capacity that a failed winner's replacement can take with no queue wait (Provisioning, Where); `maxIdle` and `ReleaseWhenIdle` bound it; and the inventory lists it (HBEP-10).
- **A hedge loser whose job started since the Provider's last read is cancelled as pending** (a stated limit; the science agent's ruling on #604's plan, Q5). Whether a loser is pending is read from its Allocation's `status.atProvider.startTime` at the cancel, so a job that started after that read is cancelled with the pending losers. It is a loser by then, so only a would-be warm spare is lost, never a winner or serving capacity.
- **A poison request kills each restarted instance.** Mitigation: the front retries a written request at most once (HBEP-5, open question 5), so a poison request kills at most two Instances per request to the front, and its give-up answer asks clients not to retry. Clients that retry anyway make new requests, which the crash-correlation event (Health and restarts) names.
- **A restart runs a different spec.** An edited script reached an older job's restart. Mitigation: each Instance's launch (command line, environment, image digest and checkpoint) is fixed in its immutable spec when it is created, and every restart reuses it (Design details).
- **Edits to pending jobs break them.** Mitigation: never edit a submitted allocation; cancel and resubmit.
- **Our own jobs compete with each other.** Hedges, over-allocations and follow-ons of different deployments compete for the same nodes and per-user limits. Mitigation: plan per provider, within its per-user QOS limits from the profile (Provisioning, Per-user limits). A submission refused for a limit waits as `SubmitLimitReached` and fails nothing (HBEP-4, QOS limits).
- **A per-user limit leaves no room for overlap.** Under Helios's QOS `now`, the cap of one GPU per user keeps a replacement from running beside its predecessor, and the owner's other projects share it; a QOS whose effective submit limit is 1 also leaves no slot to submit one. Mitigation: no-overlap mode (Handover). The predecessor serves until its drain start and drains as usual, so no request is cut; the gap shows as `NoOverlapSlot` and is measured. A deployment that must not have a gap runs on a provider whose limits allow two Instances at once.
- **The controller dies quietly.** Instances keep serving until their leases end, and handovers are missed. Mitigation: for each deployment, the condition `ReplacementLate` and an event when a replacement has not started by its predecessor's drain start minus the class's start-to-ready time (Handover).
- **A path outage looks like dead Instances.** When the dial-home path or the control-plane host's endpoint is down, every agent stops reporting at once. Mitigation: an agent that stops reporting makes its Instance unreachable, not failed; the front stops routing to it, and the controller restarts nothing on that basis alone (HBEP-6).
- **Staged files vanish.** Scratch filesystems delete old files (Helios purges files older than 30 days; spike S5, PR #19). Mitigation: the provider checks the agent's binary by digest before every submission (HBEP-4), and the agent re-stages any image or checkpoint file whose rehash fails.
- **A tunnel that never returns after a restart.** The Instance stays `ready` and ineligible at the front until its Allocation's hard end, serving nothing. Mitigation: none in alpha beyond the `TunnelConnected` condition and HBEP-6's events; it is the price of never giving up a scarce Allocation over a tunnel that may reconnect (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, for #167).

### Open questions

Each question below had a proposed answer taken from operating LLM serving on Slurm systems for a deadline-driven experiment. The owner approved the proposals on 29 Oct 2026, in a direct session recorded on PR #14, so they are decided. What that experience did not settle is marked for alpha or beta.

1. **Hedge winner milestone.**
   - **Decided:** the winner is the first Allocation whose Instance serves, not the first to start. Pending losers are cancelled only then. A loser that starts before the winner serves becomes an over-allocation candidate (question 12), not waste. This amends HBEP-3's definition. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14).
   - **Why:** start-up failures were common: broken environment builds, out-of-memory at start, collective-communication failures in new steps, scripts that killed their own stack seconds after start, engines dying minutes into use. A cancelled job's queue age is lost, and a new submission can wait days. Holding a pending loser costs no grant hours.
   - **Not needed for alpha:** hedging across more than one provider at once. Alpha submits one Allocation per Instance it needs, so the rule has one candidate and takes effect with hedging. The measured start-up failure rate per platform, which sets the hedge width, comes with it.
   - **Designed for beta** (science-agent design, 15 Nov 2026): the mechanism is Design details, Hedging (beta). A started loser's Instance ends when the winner serves, and its Allocation follows its retention policy as warm capacity, one of question 12's "rest", until that question's ranking is built. With over-allocation on, it is ranked first (Design details, Over-allocation and qualification (beta)). Beta measures the start-up failure rate; how it sets the width is decided from that measurement.
2. **Lead time and hedge width.**
   - **Decided:** plan each replacement from observed queue waits. It is submitted at the predecessor's drain start minus (the p90 of recent observed queue waits on that Provider + start-up + verification + warm-up + a margin), never earlier than the predecessor's own start. On a long queue that is the predecessor's start, as in past operation; on a short queue the replacement overlaps only by the margin. With hedging, further providers are added when the observed waits on the preferred one exceed the time left. Submissions are eligible at once, so they gain queue age. Use observed waits, never start estimates, for planning. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14), refined by the owner on 30 Oct 2026 to plan from observed waits instead of always submitting at the predecessor's start, which doubled grant hours on short queues.
   - **Why:** start estimates swung by hours and went "unknown" on every scheduler pass. Jobs with limits up to about 2.5 days started within a minute while 3-day ones waited hours. Age counts only from eligibility, so a dependency-held job lost priority to everyone else.
   - In alpha the replacement is planned this way on the predecessor's Provider (Handover). The p90 is taken over the last 20 queue waits observed there, which the Provider's status keeps (HBEP-4). With fewer than 20, the largest observed wait is used; with none, the replacement is submitted at the predecessor's start. Start-up, verification and warm-up are the class's last measured start-to-ready time, which the ModelDeployment's status keeps, or 30 min until one is measured. The margin is the profile's `handoverMargin`, 30 min by default. Source: science-agent decision, 30 Oct 2026, for the window and the fallbacks.
   - **Not needed for alpha:** submitting on further providers from observed waits, and time limits chosen to backfill. Alpha asks for the smallest of the partition's `maxTime`, the QOS's `maxWall` and the deployment's `leaseLimit` (HBEP-4, open question 11; amended 2026-11-01).
   - **Designed for beta** (science-agent design, 15 Nov 2026): when further providers are added, from which waits, and up to how many, is Design details, Hedging (beta), When a hedge grows.
   - **Decided for beta (science-agent decision, 17 Nov 2026):** no time limit is chosen to backfill, and the p90 window is the same on every platform.
     - **The backfill limit is the platform's `bf_window`,** a fact its profile records. Slurm's backfill scheduler looks that many minutes ahead, and its documentation advises "a value at least as long as the highest allowed time limit" to "prevent job starvation" (slurm.conf, `bf_window`: [24.11.5](https://slurm.schedmd.com/archive/slurm-24.11.5/slurm.conf.html), [23.11.7](https://slurm.schedmd.com/archive/slurm-23.11.7/slurm.conf.html), accessed 2026-11-17). Helios and Athena set it to 4,320 min, 3 days (`facts.slurm.scheduler_parameters` in `profiles/helios.yaml` and `profiles/athena.yaml`, both measured 30 Oct 2026). Their GPU partitions allow at most 48 h, and `plgrid-now` 1 h (`partitions[].maxTime` in `profiles/helios.yaml` and `profiles/athena.yaml`), so no time limit the plugin can ask there is longer than the window. That rests on the recorded windows alone. It doesn't answer L-1.12: that lesson's 3-day jobs, on Helios's CPU partitions, waited hours under this same 3-day window, so the window doesn't explain them, and their cause stays unknown.
     - **So Helios and Athena get no typed field and no cap.** The time limit stays the smallest of `maxTime`, `maxWall` and `leaseLimit` (HBEP-4, open question 11).
     - **Sano** allows up to 7 days on a partition. Its scheduler parameters were read and are unset: `scheduler_parameters: null`, from `scontrol show config`, measured 30 Oct 2026 on Slurm 23.11.4 (`profiles/sano.draft.yaml:16`, `:56`). With them unset, Slurm's default window applies: "Default: 1440 (1 day)" (slurm.conf, `bf_window`, [23.11.4](https://slurm.schedmd.com/archive/slurm-23.11.4/slurm.conf.html), accessed 2026-11-17). Sano's cap gets no value now. Its own reading, before a Sano partition hosts an Instance, decides it: if the window it reads is shorter than that partition's `maxTime`, the partition's time limit is capped at the window, by an amendment that types it.
     - **A limit chosen to fit the queue's holes is not used.** The holes change with the live queue, and only the scheduler's estimate would show them, which this question rules out for planning ("Use observed waits, never start estimates"). A deployment that wants shorter leases sets `leaseLimit`.
     - **The p90 window** is the last 20 waits on each Provider, on every platform. The waits are kept per Provider already (HBEP-4, Provider status, `queueWaits`), so one platform's waits never set another's plan. Nothing in the repository shows a platform whose waits need another length; beta's recorded waits would, and another length comes by amendment.
3. **Follow-on form.**
   - **Decided:** independent submissions without dependencies or node pins. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14). Alpha uses neither on any platform.
   - **Why:** jobs pinned to their predecessor's node waited for days on nodes other users had planned. Chained dependencies on jobs that could sit in the queue blocked the rest. "After N minutes" dependencies counted from the parent's start, not its end. Node and URL changes no longer matter, because Instances dial home and clients use names.
   - **Not needed for alpha:** same-node pins on platforms whose profile shows they start reliably.
4. **Overlap policy.**
   - **Decided:** keep the predecessor until its drain starts. An early release happens only under a retention policy that asks for it. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14).
   - **Why:** a design that cancelled the predecessor as soon as its follow-on answered gave back about 6 h of every 48 h allocation. Overlap capacity was used, not wasted.
5. **Warm-up.**
   - **Decided:** warm up with real-shape requests: the deployment's short, typical and longest context lengths; its typical and longest outputs; tool calls; and concurrency up to the target sessions. Warm-up is done when a repeat of each shape is not slower than the one before. It includes a corruption check that compares repeated identical requests. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14).
   - **Why:**
     - a fresh engine compiled kernels per new shape, stalling everything for 10–15 s;
     - one first request took about 600 s against 22 s a minute later;
     - an unwarmed replacement tripped a load guard on its first pass;
     - a newly joined worker stalled for 23 s;
     - long contexts were cold after short warm-ups.
   - In alpha the shapes come from the ModelDeployment's `workload` (Design details).
   - **Decided for alpha** (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from the first real-engine run, issue #94): settling judges only the shapes of fixed output length. A judged shape is not slower when its mean time is at most max(10% of the round before, 100 ms) above it. The tool-call shape still runs in every round, to warm its path, but is not judged (Instance start). This narrows the owner's "each shape" above, with the owner's approval, 1 Nov 2026 (PR #127).
   - **Not needed for alpha:** shape lists per engine version.
6. **Health and restarts.**
   - **Decided:** a malfunction gets a prompt restart; plain load does not. Malfunction signals:
     - the health endpoint fails;
     - only errors come back;
     - in-flight requests make no token progress past a threshold;
     - one worker sits at a full KV cache with requests waiting while others idle.
   - **Decided:** the restart ladder: the Instance's router (seconds; it does not touch the workers) → a worker → the whole Instance inside its Allocation → a new Allocation. A forced kill follows a few seconds after a polite stop: one router held its connections for minutes after a stop signal. After a restart, the identity check runs again, and an event says that in-flight requests were cut. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14), for both.
   - **Why:** waiting out failing servers cost hours, and restarts cleared them in seconds to minutes. Router-only restarts took about 20 s; full stack restarts took 2–4 min, up to 17.
   - In alpha the thresholds are profile values with defaults (HBEP-4). An alpha Instance has one worker and no router, so its ladder is the worker, the whole Instance, then a new Allocation (Design details).
   - **Decided for alpha** (science-agent decision, 30 Oct 2026, from the review of PR #63): `health.noProgressTimeout` is 30 min, equal to the front's `idleTimeout` (HBEP-5). A request in prefill generates no token, and the front allows a first token after 1,100 s (S2 phase A). A shorter time-out would restart a healthy worker that the front still waits on.
   - **Decided for beta (science-agent decision, 17 Nov 2026), from vLLM v0.32.0's source** (accessed 2026-11-17; HBEP-15 pins that version): a long prefill is told from a stall by the KV cache's growth. It's a second, faster rule beside the 30 min one, off until its time-out is measured. The question was opened by the science agent's decision of 30 Oct 2026, from the review of PR #63.
     - **No exported counter shows a prefill's progress** under the flags the framework passes. The scheduler emits a request's output, and its prefill statistics with it, only with new tokens, pooled output or a stop ([`vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py:2200-2212`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py#L2200-L2212)). The front end records iteration statistics only for a step with outputs ([`vllm/v1/engine/async_llm.py:852-854`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/engine/async_llm.py#L852-L854)). So `vllm:prompt_tokens_total`, `vllm:generation_tokens_total` and the histogram `vllm:iteration_tokens_total` move only when the first token comes ([`vllm/v1/metrics/loggers.py:1242-1276`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/metrics/loggers.py#L1242-L1276)), and `vllm:request_prefill_time_seconds` is observed when the request finishes (`loggers.py:1289-1301`).
     - **`vllm:kv_cache_usage_perc` moves during one.** A prefill longer than a step's token budget runs one chunk per step ([`scheduler.py:703-712`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py#L703-L712)). Chunked prefill is on by default, and `vllm serve`'s budget is 2,048 tokens a step on an A100 and 8,192 on a GH200 ([`vllm/config/scheduler.py:134`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/config/scheduler.py#L134); [`vllm/engine/arg_utils.py:2923-2949`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/engine/arg_utils.py#L2923-L2949)). Each chunk's KV blocks are allocated in its own step (`scheduler.py:778-782`), for the tokens computed so far plus the chunk's, not the whole prompt ([`vllm/v1/core/kv_cache_manager.py:541-544`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/core/kv_cache_manager.py#L541-L544), allocated at `:595-597`), and the gauge is one minus the free blocks' share ([`vllm/v1/core/block_pool.py:877-888`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/core/block_pool.py#L877-L888)). The scheduler returns its statistics every step, with outputs or none (`scheduler.py:2410-2422`; [`vllm/v1/engine/core.py:1608-1609`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/engine/core.py#L1608-L1609)), and the front end sets the gauge from them (`async_llm.py:898-903`; `loggers.py:1165`). The agent already reads it (`internal/agent/engine/metrics.go:44`).
     - **The rule.** A worker of an Instance it judges (below) is stalled when it has requests running and both its generated-token counter and its KV usage stay unchanged, across fresh samples (Health and restarts, Only fresh gauges act), for `health.stallTimeout` (proposed; HBEP-4). It's the same signal, no token progress, at the same rung. A window in which any sample shows KV usage at 0.95 or more isn't judged, since a full cache can't grow (the KV-skew signal's "full", Health and restarts). `noProgressTimeout`'s 30 min stays as the backstop.
     - **Its blind spot is one step.** A prefill that fits one step's budget, or a compile on an unseen shape inside a step (L-2.8), moves nothing until the step ends. So `stallTimeout` must exceed the longest step.
     - **Which Instances it judges** (revised by its Fable review, PR #679). The step it must outlast depends on the deployment, not only the platform. A deployment may declare its own step budget. It may turn chunked prefill off, which makes a whole prefill one step, since vLLM then raises an undeclared budget to at least the model's length ([`arg_utils.py:3140-3145`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/engine/arg_utils.py#L3140-L3145)). And it may run another engine version, since the engine is chosen per deployment (HBEP-15, Notes). So the rule judges only an Instance whose launch matches what was measured. The controller computes it from the Instance's immutable `launch` and its verification record, and every other Instance gets `noProgressTimeout` alone, so no healthy long prefill is restarted:
       - **the engine version** its verification reported (Instance start) is one that `health.stallMeasuredWith` (proposed; HBEP-4) lists, v0.32.0 once measured;
       - **chunked prefill is on:** the launch declares `enable-chunked-prefill`. Undeclared isn't enough, because vLLM turns it off by default for an encoder-decoder model and for some pooling models ([`vllm/config/model.py:2175-2218`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/config/model.py#L2175-L2218), applied at `arg_utils.py:2990-2994`), which the flags don't show;
       - **its step budget is at or below the measured one:** a declared `max-num-batched-tokens` at or below the budget measured on that partition; or none declared, where the measurement on the same partition declared none, and no `performance-mode` declared, since `throughput` doubles an undeclared budget (`arg_utils.py:3130-3134`).
     - **Its value is measured** in beta's fault-injection run (Graduation criteria, restart thresholds): on a real engine, the longest gap between two changes of the gauge during the workload's longest-context warm-up shape, sampled at `health.probeInterval`, and a forced stall that the rule catches. The run records what it was measured under in `health.stallMeasuredWith`: the engine version, the partition, the step budget and whether the deployment declared it. Unset, the rule is off, and alpha's behaviour holds. If the gauge doesn't move during a long prefill, the signal fails. The fallback is vLLM's per-step work counter, `vllm:estimated_flops_per_gpu_total`, which exists only under `--enable-mfu-metrics`, off by default ([`vllm/config/observability.py:91`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/config/observability.py#L91); [`vllm/v1/metrics/perf.py:1763-1806`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/v1/metrics/perf.py#L1763-L1806)). That launch flag would join HBEP-3's flag table with its code (proposed).
   - **Not needed for alpha:** tuning the thresholds from fault injection, which is beta; the router rung and the KV-skew signal, which need several workers.
7. **Repurposing fit.**
   - **Decided:** the fit check:
     - GPU count and memory;
     - CPU architecture (engine builds and images differ for arm64 and x86);
     - the checkpoint staged on shared storage;
     - the engine version available on the platform.
   - **Decided:** repurpose only an Allocation whose current deployment has no remaining work, reservation or fallback role, and only when the time saved exceeds the cost (stop, start, verify, warm up). A pending Allocation that starts directly as the new model costs nothing extra. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14), for both.
   - **Why:** each conversion took about 3 min to serve plus verification. Ad-hoc conversions cost hours of checks and, once, silently retired a deployment a later run needed.
   - HBEP-3 decides its open question 8: several Instances per Allocation when its resources allow; alpha places one.
   - **Not needed for alpha:** repurposing across models. Alpha moves no Allocation between ModelDeployments; a warm Allocation hosts only its own deployment's Instances (question 14).
8. **Approvals.**
   - **Decided:** needs approval:
     - releasing or cancelling a started Allocation before its hard end, outside the spec;
     - any release while a retention policy says keep.
   - **Decided:** needs none:
     - cancelling pending hedge losers;
     - releasing at the deployment's end time;
     - restarting a failing Instance;
     - handovers.
   - Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14), for both.
   - Releasing an Allocation whose Instance failed follows the spec as well: a failed Instance's Allocation is released (Over-allocation and qualification, HBEP-3). So does releasing an Allocation with `RelayEndedForGood`, whatever its retention policy, since nothing placed on it can be reached (HBEP-6, What ends a relay; #304). Source: owner, 10 Nov 2026, asked whether the controller may release such a kept Allocation without asking: "Yes, release it" (recorded on PR #331).
   - **Why:** irreversible releases needed the owner's own word, and one cancellation relayed second-hand by mistake was caught only because the owner was asked directly. Resource steps that follow agreed rules (restarts, joins, step-downs) ran without asking.
   - In alpha the controller takes no action that needs approval. Every action it takes follows the spec, and the single user changes the spec instead. From beta, HBEP-10's Operations and Approvals gate the actions outside the spec, with no webhook (HBEP-10, What needs approval; HBEP-11, Admission).
   - **Decided** (the science agent, review round of PR #582, 14 Nov 2026; HBEP-10, open question 2): in an Operation's plan, draining an Instance whose Allocation its retention then releases before its hard end counts as releasing a started Allocation before its hard end, so it needs approval. That covers `ReleaseWhenIdle`, a keep date before the hard end, and the profile's `maxIdle`. The controller's own release under `ReleaseWhenIdle`, of an Allocation emptied under the agreed spec (a handover, a failure), with no Operation, still needs none. HBEP-10 (What needs approval) lists every effect a plan can have, with its leans for the rest.
   - **Decided** (owner, 14 Nov 2026; HBEP-10, open question 3): a repurpose under question 7's rule needs no approval.
9. **Spec change while serving.**
   - **Decided:** each class's new deployment ID rolls out make-before-break, like a handover, at the next natural handover by default. It rolls out at once only when it fixes a malfunction. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14); HBEP-3, open question 7.
   - **Why:** a setting fix was applied at each stack's next restart and never as a restart of its own, so live work was not cut. Edits to files a live deployment's identity depends on held live work, so changes went into new files.
10. **Degraded mode.**
    - **Decided:** no automatic spill to a provider that is not attached to the model name. Within the attached providers the front balances as usual, capacity drops, and an event fires. An agent decides whether to attach more. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14).
    - **Why:** a gateway deployment and a self-hosted one of the same checkpoint behaved as different deployments, so mixing them silently changes results.
11. **Submission protocol and concurrency.**
    - **Decided:** one acting controller per provider, elected through a Kubernetes `Lease`, `provider-<name>` (HBEP-11). Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14). It is the Provider's controller (HBEP-4), the single writer for its provider's submissions, but never a sequential bottleneck. It follows Kubernetes controller practice:
      - **A work queue of keyed items** (one key per Allocation), processed by several workers at once. The same key is never processed by two workers at the same time; different keys run in parallel.
      - **Every reconcile is idempotent and level-triggered:** it re-reads the current state, so a repeated or reordered item does no harm.
      - **Failures requeue the item with rate-limited exponential backoff,** without blocking the other items.
      - **Writes to the control plane use optimistic concurrency** (resource versions), so parallel workers and other controllers never overwrite each other.
      - **Providers are independent shards.** Each provider's controller runs on its own, so one slow or unreachable cluster never delays another.
      - **The parallelism toward each provider is bounded:** concurrent commands share one multiplexed connection (HBEP-4).
    - The lifecycle controller follows the same practice, keyed per ModelDeployment (Design details).
    - **Decided: the submission protocol of spike S4** (issue #24, PR #32), implemented there against a fake Slurm. In hold mode it keeps at most one job per Allocation running, whatever the timing, because only the recorded job's hold is ever lifted. HBEP-3 holds its resource-model facts; HBEP-11 holds where the record lives; HBEP-4 holds the commands. Source: spike S4 (PR #32).
      - **The record.** The tag is the Allocation's external name, set before any submission (HBEP-3). The submission record and the job ID are annotations on the Allocation, each written with a resourceVersion precondition: a compare-and-swap, where a conflict means re-read, never overwrite. Status mirrors them for display (HBEP-11).
      - **Claim, then submit.** Before `sbatch`, the provider claims one attempt, numbered n, as InFlight, by compare-and-swap. A lost claim means no `sbatch`. In hold mode it then runs `sbatch --hold --no-requeue --parsable --job-name=<tag>`, with the options HBEP-4 lists.
      - **Outcomes.** Accepted: `sbatch` printed a job ID, which is written once as the job ID. Rejected: a definite refusal, or a command that never started; the next attempt waits for backoff. A refusal whose reason name is a full submit limit is one: the Allocation waits with reason `SubmitLimitReached`, and nothing fails (HBEP-4, QOS limits). Unknown: a timeout, a signal, a transport error, unparseable output or a transient reason. Unknown is never Failed. It is resolved only by finding the tag.
      - **The guard.** While an attempt is InFlight within the in-flight window, or Unknown, Observe reports the Allocation as existing, never missing, keeps a stable status message, re-checks on a short poll, and submits nothing.
      - **Adoption.** Observe always looks the tag up first: in `squeue`, then in `sacct` with an explicit start time. With no job ID recorded and jobs carrying the tag, the provider adopts one, in this order: a live job that is not held, then a job that ran (a completing job is one: HBEP-4, Core operations, Phases), then the lowest job ID. A live job that is not held comes first because only a recorded job's hold is ever lifted, so such a job proves a lost record. Jobs are sorted by job ID, never taken in output order. Adoption marks the attempt Done only when it is Unknown, or InFlight past the window; a claimant inside the window resolves its own attempt when its `sbatch` returns.
      - **Settle.** After every establish, and on every Observe while not deleting, the provider cancels every held job with the tag except the recorded one. Then it lifts the recorded job's hold once, only if all of these hold: no `hold-lifted` marker, restart count 0, never started, and reason `JobHeldUser`. It writes the marker by compare-and-swap. Immediately before the release it re-reads the marker and the job, under a per-job lock, and releases only a job that still meets all four, so a leader that stalls before that re-read cannot free a requeued job. The window after it is a known risk (HBEP-4, `LiftHold`). An admin hold is reported as a condition and never retried.
      - **Lost, and revisable.** A recorded job absent from both `squeue` and `sacct` for longer than the profile's `lostGrace` (default 2 min, above the measured gap between the two) is reported Lost, with a Warning event, and never resubmitted. If it reappears, as late accounting can make it, it returns to its real state, with an event.
      - **Hold mode** (profile `holdRelease: true`). Settle runs in the same reconcile as the submit, so the hold lasts milliseconds. On S4's fake it lasted 11 to 33 ms when the submitter lifted it, and 106 to 473 ms when a restarted controller adopted the job. A real cluster adds the `sbatch` latency and one `squeue` and one `scontrol` round trip over ssh. A held job gains no queue age, so queue age is lost only while no controller runs: 7.4 s across a `Lease` handover on the fake. Once the in-flight window has passed with no job carrying the tag in `squeue`, the attempt is dropped and the next one is n+1. A late registration lands held and is cancelled.
      - **Fallback mode** (profile `holdRelease: false`). `sbatch` runs without `--hold`, so the established job runs at once. The claim, the guard, compare-and-swap and adoption are unchanged. An InFlight claim older than the in-flight window with no job found becomes Unknown. Unknown needs positive resolution: the tag found in `squeue`, or in `sacct` with an explicit start. Elapsed time never permits a retry. Extra jobs that are not held go into a sticky duplicates list, with an event, and are cancelled on deletion.
      - **The operator condition.** In fallback mode, an attempt still Unknown after the profile's Unknown bound raises a Warning event and a condition saying that an operator must decide. The operator checks the cluster and sets `abandon-attempt` to n (HBEP-11). That marks the attempt Abandoned and allows attempt n+1.
      - **Deletion.** The finalizer stays while any job with the tag is live (held, pending, running, suspended, or completing: any state whose list still carries `COMPLETING`, HBEP-4, Core operations, Phases), or an attempt is InFlight or Unknown. Deletion cancels every live job with the tag, not only the recorded one. A submission that returns during deletion records its job ID, never lifts its hold, and is cancelled. In hold mode, a registration that arrives after the window, once the finalizer is gone, leaves a held orphan: it never runs, and HBEP-11's orphan report lists it.
      - **Alpha precondition.** `holdRelease` is verified on the alpha platform; otherwise fallback mode runs. Spike S1 (issue #18) measured it true on Helios and Athena on 30 Oct 2026, with one trial per tested partition (commit 35babef on S1's branch). Sano is not verified, so it runs fallback mode until checked (HBEP-11).
    - **Why:**
      - collisions came when several agents allocated names, ports or copies in parallel, and a single submitter per cluster kept the queue consistent;
      - too many parallel logins tripped the clusters' and a VM's connection limits, so the bound belongs to the provider;
      - hand-run sequential operations were slow; work on different deployments never needed to wait for each other.
    - **From spike S4 (PR #32):** for Slurm the tag is in the job name (HBEP-3), because accounting keeps the job comment only where a site enables it. The `Lease` is an extra guard. It does not prevent duplicate jobs, since a deposed leader's submission can still land; in hold mode, holding every job and lifting only the recorded job's hold does (HBEP-11). The tag lookup finds jobs and resolves Unknown attempts.
    - Each platform's `inflightWindow` is set above the `sbatch` latency over ssh, which is measured before alpha (HBEP-4, Graduation criteria).
    - **Not needed for alpha:**
      - the worker count and the bounds per platform, from measurement; alpha uses code defaults (HBEP-4, Transport);
      - detecting a hold that a site's `job_submit` plugin drops at submission. It stays a documented hazard. S4 tried an event for it and removed it, because a peer's legitimate lift of the hold raced with it and gave false alarms. Alpha relies on `holdRelease` measured on each alpha partition.
12. **Over-allocation and qualification.**
    - **Decided:** qualify every Instance, also when k is 0. The checks:
      - health, engine version, model names and maximum length;
      - a chat request with its engine fingerprint;
      - a tool-call smoke test;
      - KV capacity from start-up;
      - a corruption check;
      - a short behaviour check on the workload's own long-context shape (malformed or looping tool calls);
      - throughput at the target sessions against absolute gates from the platform profile, such as ms per token and time to first token.
    - **Decided:** start with k = 1 per provider and adjust it from the measured qualification failure rate. Rank by throughput at the target sessions. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14), for both.
    - **Why:** the same checkpoint behaved differently per node and per deployment. One deployment failed a malfunction check that its twin passed, and malfunctions showed mostly on long loops. Measured KV per session was twice the estimate.
    - In alpha k is 0, and every Instance passes every check but the throughput gate. Throughput at the target sessions is measured and recorded. It gates nothing, because no profile holds expected performance yet, and a reference must not come from the Instances it judges (HBEP-4, Test plan).
    - **Not needed for alpha:** k above 0, the best-N ranking, and the benchmark length per platform.
    - **Designed for beta** (science-agent design, 16 Nov 2026): the mechanism is Design details, Over-allocation and qualification (beta). Expected performance is a profile entry per deployment ID, capacity flags and measured shape, from a reference run apart from serving, with HBEP-4's envelope (HBEP-4, open question 3 and Platform profile schema). The benchmark is warm-up's settled round, as in alpha, so no benchmark length per platform is needed. Beta records the qualification failure rate and does not yet adjust k.
    - **Decided** (owner, 15 Nov 2026, asked in the science agent's terminal): **k = 1 stays every deployment's default** ("Yes, default k = 1"), at the grant-hour cost stated under Over-allocation and qualification (beta), and `overAllocation: 0` opts a deployment out. **An Allocation whose Instance fails the throughput gate alone, twice, is released** ("Release it"), as the decided rule for a failed check says (open question 8).
    - **Decided for beta** (science agent, 15 Nov 2026): no extra with planned replacements, since the ranking runs once per deployment's first placement; beta's measurement of the qualification failure rate decides whether replacements need one. The gate's numbers (the 100% tolerance, the 90-day age of an entry, and the nodes a reference run measures) are proposals, revisited from beta's measurement.
    - **Open, decided by a measurement:** in host-dialed mode, a third Allocation per Provider, which HBEP-4's open question 17 (the login nodes' measured budget) decides: by its rule, a third needs 6 relays per login host. Until then a deployment that hands over gets no extra there.
13. **Default retention policy.**
    - **Decided:** keep until the deployment's end time, or until the grant's end or the deadline when one is set. Release-when-idle is opt-in, and a release needs certainty that no reader, reservation, fallback or re-run needs the Allocation. This reverses the earlier candidate, "release when idle". Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14).
    - **Why:** a released slot went to another user within seconds, a new submission started with no queue age, and Allocations kept through the deadline were repurposed as plans changed. Releases before new capacity was running prevented that reuse.
    - **Decided for alpha (science-agent decision, 30 Oct 2026):** Athena's profile sets `maxIdle` to 30 min, because its documentation warns that workloads not using GPUs lead to account suspension, and an idle GPU Allocation may count as one. Other platforms leave it unset. **Decided for beta (owner, 17 Nov 2026, recorded on #679):** Athena's `maxIdle` stays 30 min, and Cyfronet's answer on idle GPU jobs no longer gates beta. If it comes, it's a profile change. Before, that answer was open, blocking beta.
14. **Placing Instances on warm capacity.**
    - **Decided:** the controller may place a replacement or an added Instance of the same deployment on a warm Allocation by itself. Moving an Allocation to a different model is a repurpose, decided by an operator under question 7's cost rule. Source: owner, 29 Oct 2026 (PR #14).
    - **Why:** idle capacity was given work at once. Model changes needed a stated gain.
    - In alpha the warm Allocation must be in the class the Instance needs, with lease left above twice that class's last measured start-to-ready time (30 min until one is measured) plus the drain window.

## Design details

### Controllers

- **The lifecycle controller** is one controller-runtime manager. Alpha runs one replica, which the launcher restarts when it exits (HBEP-11, open question 6). It takes a `Lease` named `lifecycle`, in the namespace `hpcburst-system`, which the launcher creates, as a guard against a stale second copy. Run with `--leader-elect=false`, a development and test mode, it holds no `Lease`. Its `/readyz` fails until its caches have synced and, with leader election on, it leads that `Lease`; its `/healthz` is liveness only (HBEP-8, Observers). Every write it makes, its agent endpoint's included, names the field manager `hpcburst-lifecycle` (HBEP-11).
- **Its work queue is keyed per ModelDeployment.** Several workers run at once (four by default), and one key is never processed by two at once. It watches ModelDeployments, Allocations, Instances, Providers and PlatformProfiles. Each event maps to a ModelDeployment's key: an Allocation through its `model-deployment` label, an Instance through its owner, a Provider or PlatformProfile to every deployment that allows it. In beta it also follows Operations, each mapped to the key of the ModelDeployment it acts on, and Approvals, through their Operation (HBEP-10, Operations).
- **What it writes:**
  - Allocations: it creates and deletes them, and writes their `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/idle-since` annotation, the idle anchor (Retention). It also sets the queued-successor mark, the annotation `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/queued-successor` (proposed), in the create request of an Allocation that needs it, and never changes it after (Handover, No-overlap mode). From beta, before each delete it writes the annotation `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/release-cause` (Release). The Provider's controller writes the status and the record's other annotations, and only reads the mark (HBEP-11);
  - Instances: it creates and deletes them, writes their phase, conditions, `status.leaseEnd`, `status.endTime`, `status.restarts` and `status.agent`, and writes restart generations as annotations. In beta it also sets the hedge annotation, `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/hedge-group` (proposed), in the create request of a hedge's later member, and never changes it after (Hedging), and ends a ranked-out Instance with `RankedOut` (Over-allocation and qualification (beta)). On every observe (Reconcile loop, step 3) it copies the Allocation's hard end into the Instance's `status.leaseEnd`, from which the front enforces the drain deadline (HBEP-5). A later hard end, once observed, is copied too, and moves the drain start;
  - Providers: only the condition `DialHomeUnreachable`, by server-side apply to the status under `hpcburst-lifecycle`, owning that one entry (The allocation agent; HBEP-4, open question 16);
  - identity records, and through its verification controller the verification records (HBEP-11);
  - ModelDeployment status, including each class's last measured start-to-ready time (Handover), in beta each class's start-up outcomes (Hedging) with each one's gate verdict (Over-allocation and qualification (beta)), and a managed source's status (Reconcile loop, step 1);
  - in beta, a ModelDeployment's spec only to execute an Operation: a lower `spec.instances` for a `Scale`, and `spec.endTime` set to now, or the delete, for an `End` (Reconcile loop, Operations; HBEP-10);
  - in beta, Operation status: the phase, the plan, the committed targets and the per-target outcomes. The Provider's controller fills in the start estimate (HBEP-10, The plan; HBEP-4, `Estimate`);
  - Events.
- **It never calls a provider.** The Provider's controller acts on each Allocation (HBEP-4; open question 11).
- **It serves the agent endpoint** for the allocation agents (The allocation agent).
- **Verification** (HBEP-9) runs as its own controller inside the same manager in alpha, as HBEP-11 places it (Controllers). It writes the verification records.

### What the controller reads

- **From the ModelDeployment** (HBEP-11): the model name, the source (a serving spec, or a managed catalog entry), the number of instances, the allowed providers, the end time and the retention policy. In alpha the allowed providers are tried in the order their names are listed, then those that only the label selector matches, by name.
- **From the ModelDeployment's `routing`** (HBEP-5): `routing.maxRequestDuration`, which shortens its Instances' drain window (Handover). The front reads the rest of `routing`.
- **Fields this HBEP adds to the ModelDeployment's spec** (an amendment to HBEP-11's API):
  - `leaseLimit` (duration, optional): the longest time limit of its Allocations, when shorter than the partition's `maxTime`.
  - `workload`: the shapes for warm-up and qualification.
    - `contextTokens.typical`, `outputTokens.typical` and `targetSessions` are required. `targetSessions` is at most 1024, so a report at the bounds fits its size limit (Reports).
    - `contextTokens.short` defaults to 512 tokens, and `outputTokens.longest` to 4,096 tokens.
    - `contextTokens.longest` defaults to the resolved context limit (the `max-model-len` the engine reports) minus `outputTokens.typical`, the output paired with it, minus the template overhead. It is therefore fixed at verification. The overhead is measured by rendering an empty conversation (one user message with no content, plus the generation prompt) through the engine's chat template. vLLM counts input and output tokens against one limit (`max_total_tokens` in [`vllm/renderers/params.py` at v0.32.0](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/renderers/params.py), accessed 2026-10-30), so a prompt that fills the context leaves no room for the answer.
    - **Every shape must fit.** The shapes are each context length with the typical output, and the typical context with the longest output (Instance start). A shape that does not fit is an error, never shortened. When the serving spec declares `max-model-len`, the controller checks the declared shapes against it before it provisions, and a shape that does not fit sets `Degraded` with reason `SpecUnsupported`. At verification each rendered request, overhead included, is checked against the context limit the engine reports, and one that does not fit fails verification and stops provisioning in that class (Instance start). That stop is keyed on the class's deployment ID plus a hash of the resolved shapes, so a workload edit that makes the shapes fit lifts it at the next reconcile, without a change to the serving spec. The behaviour check of a reasoning model runs at a shorter prompt (Instance start); the shapes themselves are never shortened.
    - `toolCalls` defaults to whether the serving spec enables automatic tool choice.
  - `hedgeWidth` (integer, optional; beta, proposed): the most Providers one hedge spans at once, 2 by default, at least 1. With 1 nothing is hedged. A lower value applies to members added after it and cancels none (Hedging).
  - `overAllocation` (integer, optional; beta, proposed): k, the extra Allocations per Provider, 1 by default; 0 turns over-allocation off, and beta admits no more than 1 (Over-allocation and qualification (beta)).
- **From the PlatformProfile** (HBEP-4): per partition, the accelerator class, `maxTime`, `gpusPerNode`, `cpusPerGpu`, `memPerGpu`, `imageFormat` and its own QOS (`qos`, proposed). For the platform: `drainWindow`, `drainMargin`, `handoverMargin`, `maxIdle`, the `health` and `restart` groups, and the QOS list with its limits (`qos`, proposed; HBEP-4, QOS limits). In beta, `expectedPerformance` (proposed; HBEP-4, Platform profile schema), the throughput gate's references.
- **From the Provider's spec** (HBEP-4): its `qos` (proposed), whose limits, with its partition QOS's, bound its Allocations (Provisioning, Per-user limits).
- **From the Provider's status** (HBEP-4): `queueWaits`, the last 20 observed queue waits, which plan replacements (Handover). For a managed Provider, its `catalog` and its `CredentialsRejected` condition, which a managed-source ModelDeployment's `Ready` follows (Reconcile loop, step 1).
- **The Instance's spec** gains `launch` (an amendment to HBEP-11's API). It is the command line and environment rendered from the serving spec, the image reference and digest, the checkpoint repository, revision and identity record, and the GPU count. It is written when the Instance is created, and it is immutable like the rest of the spec. Every start and restart of that Instance runs exactly this; with several workers, each worker's start is rendered from it (Several workers).
  - The command line always carries `--max-num-seqs`, from `launch.maxNumSeqs`: the serving spec's value, else 256, a framework default. So the front's per-Instance cap is always known (HBEP-5). `max-num-seqs` is capacity, not a deployment-ID input (HBEP-3's table), so the default changes no ID.

### Reconcile loop

One pass for one ModelDeployment. Every step is idempotent and re-reads what it acts on.
1. **Read** the ModelDeployment, its allowed Providers in order, and their PlatformProfiles.
   - **A served ModelDeployment** ignores the managed Providers in its selection, even when their labels match, as the front does (HBEP-5). A batch Provider that is missing or not `Ready` is skipped, with a condition naming it.
   - **A managed-source ModelDeployment** (`spec.source.managed`) is provisioned nothing: no Allocation and no Instance, whatever `spec.instances` says. The controller writes its status and stops here; the front routes to the catalog entry (HBEP-5). Its status says that it has no Instances by design: `desiredInstances` and `readyInstances` are 0, and `Progressing` is false with reason `ManagedSource`. `Ready` follows the catalog entry. It is true while an allowed managed Provider's status lists the entry's catalog name now and that Provider's `CredentialsRejected` is not true. Otherwise it is false, with reason `CatalogEntryUnlisted` or `CredentialsRejected`. When no allowed managed Provider carries `spec.source.managed.platform` as its platform label, it is false with reason `ProviderNotOnPlatform`, and the front routes the entry to no Provider off that platform (HBEP-5).
2. **Current IDs.** For each accelerator class that the allowed partitions offer, compute the class's current ID from the serving spec (HBEP-3), and create its identity record if absent (HBEP-11). Status lists the IDs by class. No Allocation is created for a class without a current ID.
   - When the ID cannot be computed, the ModelDeployment gets `Degraded` with reason `IdentityUnresolved`, whose message names the cause. The causes and their retry rules are HBEP-3's (Deployment ID, "When the ID cannot be computed", and the collision rule): transient Hub failures are retried with backoff and set no reason; a permanent Hub failure is evaluated again when the spec or token changes and at resync; a collision is evaluated again only when the spec or the identity records change, so it does not loop.
3. **Observe** its Allocations (by the `model-deployment` label), its Instances (by owner), and each agent's latest report.
4. **Shutdown.** At the end time, on deletion, or at zero desired instances, apply Release's shutdown case and stop here.
5. **Failed Allocations.** An Allocation whose provider phase is `Failed` (a requeue included), `Lost` or `Refused`, or which has the condition `SubmittedAsOther` or `RelayEndedForGood` (HBEP-6, What ends a relay; #304), ends its Instance with that reason and is deleted; its finalizer cancels whatever carries its tag (HBEP-4). A `Lost` job is absent from the queue listing, so it is neither pending nor running. An Allocation that is `Ended` while its Instance is not `ended` ends the Instance with reason `AllocationEndedEarly`, raises a `failing` event with that reason, and is deleted; step 7 then provisions the replacement. An Instance already past its hard end ends as Handover's draining says, without that reason.
6. **Instances.** Advance each Instance through its phases (Instance start), apply the health rules (Health and restarts) and the drain deadline (Handover). In beta, a hedge whose member became `ready` is resolved in the same pass (Hedging), and an Instance that passed its checks becomes `ready` when the deployment's selection of primaries chooses it, and is otherwise ranked (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), How it counts and The ranking point).
7. **Capacity.** A primary Instance is one that is not draining or ended, and that has no predecessor still counted. In beta the members of one hedge count once, as one primary or as one replacement (Hedging). With over-allocation on, the primaries are the `spec.instances` most advanced of those, and the rest are surplus (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), How it counts).
   - Fewer primaries than desired: create one Allocation and its Instance, in phase `pending`, per missing primary, within the Provider's per-user limits (Provisioning). In beta the pass then creates one extra on each Provider where it created new Allocations for missing primaries (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), k, and when the extra is submitted).
   - More primaries than desired: scale down. Delete pending Allocations first, newest first; then drain the ready primaries with the nearest hard end. A surplus primary's replacement, if any, is deleted with it, never promoted. A hedge with no winner is one primary, ranked by its most advanced member: pending while no member's job has started, else starting. It goes whole, and each member ends by its own state (Hedging, Interactions). While an Operation is `Executing` on the ModelDeployment, the scale-down takes that Operation's committed targets instead of choosing its own (Operations, below). With over-allocation on, the scale-down takes the surplus, in the order How it counts gives (Over-allocation and qualification (beta)).
8. **Replacements.** Every primary whose Allocation is `Running`, that has no live replacement, and whose replacement's planned submission time has come gets one (Handover), when an allowed Provider's per-user limits leave room for it. When none does, the deployment runs in no-overlap mode: on the one-slot path step 7 creates the successor once the slot is free, and on the queued path this step creates it at its planned time to pend behind the per-user cap (Handover, No-overlap mode). In beta, a hedge with no winner, a missing primary's or a replacement's, gets its next member when the hedge rule says (Hedging, When a hedge grows). In beta a replacement never waits for a ranking (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), How it counts).
9. **Retention.** Apply each empty Allocation's retention policy (Retention), and place Instances on warm Allocations where question 14 allows.
10. **Status.** Write the ready and desired counts, the current IDs, every ID that still has Instances, each class's last measured start-to-ready time, the conditions, and the generation reconciled with its time.
11. **Requeue** the key at the earliest coming deadline (a drain start, a replacement's planned submission time or lateness deadline, a hedge member's hedge time, a registration or start timeout, a retention end), and after 30 s at the latest.

**Operations** (beta; HBEP-10, Operations). An Operation acts on one ModelDeployment and runs in that ModelDeployment's pass, so one worker holds the key, and executing a plan never races the controller's own handover. In the pass:
- **Plan.** The controller writes the Operation's plan from the state the pass has read, with no side effects, using the code that executes: a `Scale` plan runs step 7's scale-down selection. It writes the Operation's status only when the plan or the phase changes.
- **Commit.** Once the owner has confirmed the plan's ID and each target that needs approval has its Approvals, the pass recomputes the plan once. A different ID turns the Operation `Stale`, and nothing runs. Otherwise one status write records the targets, each with its UID, generation and effect, and sets `phase: Executing`. The controller never re-plans after that.
- **Act.** The pass acts on exactly the committed targets, with a UID precondition on every delete. A `Scale` cancels or drains them and lowers `spec.instances` in the same pass. An `End` sets `spec.endTime` to now, or deletes the ModelDeployment, and step 4's shutdown applies, its committed targets being everything the shutdown takes (Release, approved early termination). A `Release` deletes the committed Allocations. A target already done is skipped, one whose UID is gone counts as done, and a failed one is retried with backoff.
- **A restart** resumes from the committed targets in status, never from a new plan.

### Provisioning

- **Where.** A new primary goes to the first allowed Provider, in order, that is `Ready`, is batch, whose partition's class has a current ID, whose time limit can host an Instance (A partition too short), and whose per-user limits leave a slot (Per-user limits). A replacement goes to its predecessor's Provider first, which keeps the ID, and otherwise to the next one in order. In beta a hedge adds further Providers by the same checks, and warm Allocations of the deployment come into the order where open question 14 allows them (Hedging; revised by the Fable review of PR #602):
  - **a replacement** goes first to its predecessor's Provider when it has a slot, which keeps the ID (a warm Allocation there is used before a new submission, as today); then to a warm Allocation of the same class on any allowed Provider, in order; then to a warm Allocation of another class; then to a new submission in the allowed order;
  - **a missing primary** goes first to a warm Allocation of its deployment, of any class, on the allowed Providers in order; then to a new submission in the allowed order.
- **The Allocation's submission parameters** (HBEP-4, Submission):
  - `partition`: the Provider's one allowed partition;
  - `gpus`: `tensor-parallel-size` times `pipeline-parallel-size` from the serving spec, each 1 when absent. It must not exceed the smaller of `gpusPerNode`, since alpha Instances are single-node, and the QOS's `maxGpusPerUser` when set (HBEP-4, QOS limits). A larger count is refused on that Provider, never capped, because it is the engine's parallel size; with no allowed Provider left, the deployment has `Degraded` reason `SpecUnsupported`. A `data-parallel-size` above 1 is refused, with `Degraded` reason `SpecUnsupported` and no Allocation, because the framework runs its workers itself. In beta, `gpus` is `spec.workersPerInstance` times that product (Several workers);
  - `cpusPerTask` and `memory`: `gpus` times `cpusPerGpu` and `memPerGpu`, each capped at the QOS's `maxCpusPerUser` and `maxMemPerUser` when set. They are an allotment in proportion, so a cap shrinks them rather than refusing the Allocation. Neither cap changes an alpha request (HBEP-4, Submission, GPU sizing);
  - `timeLimit`: the smallest of the partition's `maxTime`, the QOS's `maxWall` and `leaseLimit` (HBEP-4, QOS limits). Under Helios's `now` it is 12 h;
  - `nodes`: 1;
  - `agentDigest`: the allocation agent's build for the partition's `cpuArch`.
- **Its retention policy** is the ModelDeployment's.
- **Its credential.** The controller generates a random 256-bit token per Allocation and keeps it in a mode-600 file on the control-plane host, named by the Allocation's UID (HBEP-11, Credentials). The Provider's controller places it on the platform (HBEP-4).
- **The Instance** is created with its Allocation, with the class's current ID and its `launch`. In alpha an Allocation's assignment never changes, so both exist from the start.
- **No front, no submission.** While no front address and dial-home mode are configured, no Allocation is submitted, and the ModelDeployment has `Degraded` with reason `FrontNotConfigured` (HBEP-11, Single-user host).
- **A Rejected submission** is retried by the Provider's controller with backoff (HBEP-4). The lifecycle controller marks the deployment degraded, with Slurm's reason. It does not switch providers by itself in alpha. A refusal for a full submit limit, told by Slurm's reason name, is not a failure: its Allocation stays `Submitting` with reason `SubmitLimitReached` (HBEP-4, QOS limits), and the deployment shows `SubmitLimitReached` in place of `SubmissionRejected`. Any other policy refusal stays `SubmissionRejected`, with its reason name in the message.
- **Per-user limits** (science-agent decision, 1 Nov 2026). The controller plans per Provider within the submit limit of the QOS its Allocations run under, `maxSubmitPerUser` (HBEP-4, QOS limits). It counts every Allocation of that Provider, of every ModelDeployment: held, pending or running, and also `Submitting`, `SubmissionUnknown` and being deleted, from its creation until its phase is `Ended` and its `jobCompleting` (proposed; HBEP-4, Core operations, Phases) is false, or its deletion completes. On an Allocation being deleted, `jobCompleting` also covers another tagged job that is still completing, such as a cancelled duplicate (HBEP-4's amendment of 2 Nov 2026). Counting a completing job is stricter than Slurm on purpose: Slurm frees the submit count and the per-user caps when it sets the flag, but the controller keeps the slot until the flag clears, so one rule says when a job is live (HBEP-4, Phases). It creates no Allocation on a Provider whose count has reached the limit. The limit is the one Slurm applies: for each limit, the partition QOS's value if set, unless the job's QOS has `OverPartQOS`, else the Provider's `qos`'s; with no `qos`, only the partition QOS's typed limits (HBEP-4, QOS limits). On Helios `plgrid-gpu-gh200` under `now` that is the partition QOS's 2,000 jobs, with `now`'s cap of one GPU per user.
  - A replacement can run beside its predecessor only when the count leaves a slot for it and the two together fit the QOS's per-user caps on GPUs, CPUs and memory. When either fails, the deployment runs in no-overlap mode (Handover).
  - **Slots are reserved atomically** (science-agent decision, 1 Nov 2026, from PR #151's review). Before it creates an Allocation, the lifecycle controller reserves a slot under a lock per Provider, which every worker takes. The count it checks is the listed Allocations of that Provider that have not ended, an `Ended` one whose `jobCompleting` is true counting as not ended, plus the creations it has made that its cache does not show yet. A creation that fails gives its slot back, and one that the cache shows is then counted once, as listed. This is the expectations pattern of Kubernetes' ReplicaSet controller: "Expectations are a way for controllers to tell the controller manager what they expect", raised by `ExpectCreations`, lowered by `CreationObserved`, and expired after `ExpectationsTimeout`, 5 min ([`pkg/controller/controller_utils.go` at v1.35.0, lines 72, 127–169 and 236–266](https://github.com/kubernetes/kubernetes/blob/v1.35.0/pkg/controller/controller_utils.go#L127-L169), accessed 2026-11-01). With one active replica, no two workers create past the limit. Across a leader hand-over one extra creation can still race, since a `Lease` does not fence (HBEP-11). Slurm's own limit, or the GPU cap, then handles it: a refused submission is `SubmitLimitReached`, and an accepted one pends.
  - **A serving primary's replacement slot is reserved** (owner decision, 15 Nov 2026, asked in the science agent's terminal: "Reserve the handover slot"; the owner added: "Can always reserve the slots without my permission"). A primary that is `Running`, whose replacement the controller would create (Handover: the deployment has no end time, or one after the primary's drain start), and that has no live replacement yet, holds one slot on its Provider for that replacement. In beta, so does a hedge's winner on a Provider other than its predecessor's, from its win, when the controller would create its replacement (the deployment has no end time, or one after the winner's drain start), and while it has no live replacement (Hedging, Interactions; revised by the Fable review of PR #602). The count a new Allocation checks is the Allocations counted above plus those reserved slots, so a new primary, of any deployment, is created only when a slot is left beyond them. A replacement takes its predecessor's reserved slot.
    - **In host-dialed mode,** where a Provider has at most 2 Allocations (HBEP-6), this means one serving deployment per Provider: a second deployment's primary waits, with `SubmitLimitReached` naming the deployment whose handover holds the slot, until the first ends or no longer needs a replacement.
    - **In no-overlap mode,** where the effective submit limit is 1, the slot freed when a predecessor ends goes to its successor before any other deployment's primary.
    - **A deployment's own second primary** counts the same way: in host-dialed mode, a serving primary and its reserved replacement fill a Provider, so `instances: 2` needs a second Provider for the second primary (science-agent ruling on #599, 15 Nov 2026).
    - **A replacement that can't be placed for a lasting cause ends,** so it doesn't hold its slot indefinitely: its Provider dropped from the deployment's list or managed, a partition too short for its lease, or its sizing refused. It ends with the existing reasons (`AllocationLost`, `SpecUnsupported`). The primary's reserved slot then returns, and the replacement is planned again, on its Provider first and then the others. A Provider problem, which passes, doesn't end it (science-agent ruling on #599).
    - **Two replacements on one Provider with an effective limit of 1,** possible only through a leader hand-over race, would each count the other's slot. The older one, by creation time and then UID, takes the slot, and the other waits (science-agent ruling on #599).
    - **A hedge** (beta): its member on the predecessor's Provider takes the reserved slot, and a member on another Provider takes only a slot left beyond that Provider's reserved slots (Hedging, Interactions).
    - **Why:** a second deployment created on a Provider whose slots a serving deployment needs would block that deployment's replacement, so its capacity would end at its lease's hard end, and getting it back can take days (HBEP-10, Motivation).
  - The owner's other projects, and a second Provider on the same login, share the same limits but are not counted. A refusal they cause is `SubmitLimitReached` too, and a GPU they hold makes our job pend.
  - When a missing primary finds no Provider with a slot, no Allocation is created, and the deployment has `Degraded` with reason `SubmitLimitReached`, naming each Provider and its limit. That is the case when desired instances exceed what the limits allow, such as two Instances on a QOS whose effective submit limit is 1. In no-overlap mode `NoOverlapSlot` ranks before it, so during the gap the condition carries `NoOverlapSlot`, and a successor that is held back or refused adds `SubmitLimitReached` to the message (Events and conditions).
- **A partition too short** (science-agent decision, 1 Nov 2026). An Allocation whose time limit is not longer than the lead an Instance needs cannot host one. The lead is the Instance's drain window (Handover: `drainWindow`, or `routing.maxRequestDuration` plus `drainMargin` when that is shorter) plus `handoverMargin` plus `health.startTimeout`. Such a Provider is skipped, as for a GPU count it cannot host, and no Allocation is created on it. The deployment has `Degraded` with reason `PartitionTooShort`, whose message names the partition, the time limit, the bound that set it (`maxTime`, the QOS's `maxWall`, or `leaseLimit`) and the lead needed. Athena's `plgrid-now` is the example: its time limit is 1 h, and the lead is 3 h 40 min with the profile's defaults, or 1 h 45 min even with a 5 min `routing.maxRequestDuration`. Helios `plgrid-gpu-gh200` under `now` gives 12 h against 3 h 40 min, and a 4 h `leaseLimit` with a 20 min `routing.maxRequestDuration` gives 4 h against 2 h. Time limits are whole minutes, so the boundary is tested in minutes. Athena's `plgrid-now`, 1 h, is too short under `profiles/athena.yaml`. The shipped test profile `profiles/athena-now.yaml`, with minutes-long `routing.maxRequestDuration`, `drainMargin`, `handoverMargin` and `health.startTimeout`, gives a lead under 1 h, so short test deployments run there (owner decision, 11 Nov 2026; HBEP-4, open question 8).
- **The hedge winner** is the first Allocation whose Instance serves (open question 1). In alpha each missing Instance has one Allocation, so the rule decides nothing. Beta builds it (Hedging).

### The allocation agent

The process that an Allocation's job runs. It is a static binary, one build per CPU architecture, staged by digest (HBEP-4). It is a new term, added to HBEP-3's vocabulary by amendment. It is the framework's in-allocation half; the controller decides, and the agent carries out.
- **Start.** The batch script's bootstrap sets `TMPDIR` from the partition's `tmpDir` (a proposed profile field), checks the binary's digest and runs it with the Allocation's directory (HBEP-4, Submission). Nothing else runs in the job. So "the job's private temporary directory" below is under the `TMPDIR` the bootstrap set: `$MEMFS` on Helios GH200, with the `memfs` constraint: a RAM disk whose contents count against the job's `--mem`, where the job's own `TMPDIR` is on Lustre (HBEP-4, Submission; Platform profile schema).
- **Registration.** The agent connects to the control-plane host over HBEP-6's dial-home path. In alpha that is direct on the dial-home port, or through ssh on the configured ssh port, 22 by default, with a restricted key (HBEP-11, open question 8).
  - It presents the Allocation's credential, read from its mode-600 file, with its job ID, its node's CPU architecture and its GPUs.
  - The endpoint accepts it only when the credential is the Allocation's and the job ID is the one the provider recorded. That binds the registration to the provider's identity for the job (HBEP-6).
  - **Its identity** (science-agent decision, 31 Oct 2026). Each agent process draws a random `agentInstanceID` at its start, 32 lowercase hex characters, and sends it on every call. The registration also carries the scheduler's `restartCount` of the job, from `SLURM_RESTART_COUNT`, 0 when the scheduler sets none. The controller keeps both in the Instance's `status.agent`. A registration from the job the Instance was started under, with another `agentInstanceID` or a higher `restartCount`, is a second execution of the job: a requeue (Health and restarts). The kernel's boot ID is never used, because a requeued job can land on the same node without a reboot.
  - **Registration is soft state** (science-agent decision, 31 Oct 2026). The controller keeps registrations in memory. After its own restart it answers each agent's next call as not registered, and the agent registers again at once, within one poll, with the same `agentInstanceID`. That is the same execution: no Instance fails and no generation is raised. Until a long poll and a report interval have passed since its start, a restarted controller judges neither a registration timeout nor `AgentUnreachable`.
  - It reads the control-plane host's address, the dial-home mode, the pin of the agent endpoint's certificate, and the absolute staging and dial-home directories from `agent.json` in its directory (HBEP-4, Submission).
  - An agent that has not registered within `health.registrationTimeout` (15 min by default) of its job's start fails its Instance, with reason `RegistrationTimeout`, and the Allocation is released.
  - **In host-dialed mode** (HBEP-6, Dial-home modes, 9 Nov 2026):
    - **Its listeners:** the agent binds two loopback listeners at its start, control and serving, and keeps them for the Allocation's life. frpc's `serverPort` is the serving listener's port, and the control client uses the control listener.
    - **The rendezvous:** it creates a Unix socket in a directory with a random name and mode 0700, under its `$TMPDIR` or `/dev/shm`, and publishes the socket's absolute path (at most 107 bytes) in `<Allocation directory>/relay-socket`, mode 600, by rename. It accepts only a peer of its own user.
    - **The sessions:** `relay <channel>`, run in an `srun --overlap` step by the batch Provider's controller (in beta, by the relay keeper on its desired set), writes its preamble, connects to that socket, and hands over its standard input and output. The agent runs each channel's session over it.
    - **Which session wins:** it keeps the session with the highest epoch and sequence, and refuses a lower one as `superseded`.
    - **A connection that finds no session** waits up to 30 s.
  - In ssh mode the agent keeps two forwards itself, `ssh -N -L` over the configured ssh port, 22 by default, each in its own reconnect loop, as spike S1's kit does. The port is configurable because the development VM's sshd is reached through a port forward on a gateway, at the gateway's address and port (owner decision, 31 Oct 2026; HBEP-6). Connection 1 forwards to the agent endpoint's loopback port, for the agent's control traffic. Connection 2 forwards to the front's dial-home listener's loopback port, for frpc's tunnel (HBEP-6). Two connections keep a large report off the token tunnel, since streams sharing one connection block each other (HBEP-6, Risks). Each local end is a loopback port the kernel chooses once, at the agent's start, and the agent keeps it for the Allocation's life. frpc starts only once connection 2's forward listens. If a port cannot be bound again and a new one is taken, the agent points its control client at it (connection 1), or rewrites frpc's configuration and restarts frpc (connection 2) (HBEP-6, Dial-home modes). Both use one dedicated, restricted key that the operator generated on the platform once, so that its private half never travels, and a pinned host key. Both are read by path, mode 600, from `~/.hpcburst/dial-home/` on the cluster, whose absolute path `agent.json` carries. They live outside `stagingDir`, on storage that is not purged, because Helios purges scratch files older than 30 days (spike S5, PR #19). The Provider's controller checks that they exist before any submission, and sets `DialHomeKeyMissing` otherwise (HBEP-4). When an Instance's registration over the dial-home path fails, a home directory that compute nodes cannot read included, the lifecycle controller sets `DialHomeUnreachable` on the Provider (HBEP-4, open question 16). The key's line in `authorized_keys` on the control-plane host (owner decision, 31 Oct 2026), one key per cluster, is `restrict,port-forwarding,permitopen="127.0.0.1:<agent endpoint port>",permitopen="127.0.0.1:<dial-home listener port>",permitlisten="127.0.0.1:1",command="/bin/false"`. The two ports are the launcher's port base + 3 and + 7: the agent endpoint and the front's dial-home listener, which in ssh mode defaults to loopback (`dialHomeListen`, HBEP-5). No ssh forward from a compute node has run yet (HBEP-6).
- **Desired state, pulled.** The agent long-polls for what its Allocation should run now: nothing, one Instance with its `launch` and the restart generation of each part, or stop. For that Instance it also says whether to verify, qualify or warm up now, with the identity record's file list and the workload's shapes. It converges on that state, as a kubelet converges on its pods, so a missed message is corrected by the next poll.
  - **The start instruction carries the file list** (science-agent ruling, 31 Oct 2026, for #87). For a first start and for a rung-2 restage, the desired state for that Instance generation carries `Checks.Verify` with `Files`. The agent starts no worker without it, so a worker never starts on unstaged files. It reports staging in progress with a message that the file list is awaited, and an Instance whose list never comes fails at `health.startTimeout` (Instance start). A worker restart (rung 1) compares settings only, so its `Checks.Verify` carries no files.
- **Reports.** At least every 15 s, and at every change, the agent posts:
  - for each part: its phase, process ID and start time, exit code, and the restart generation it applied; for a worker that exited without being told to, when it exited by the agent's clock (`ExitedAt`), on which crash correlation is judged (Health and restarts); and for an engine whose start failed, its log, cut short like any free-text field;
  - staging progress. The agent's Hub GETs send `Accept-Encoding: identity`, so the Hub sends an exact `Content-Length`, and the total and the bytes done are byte-exact. A response that still lacks `Content-Length` leaves that file's total unknown, never guessed: `bytesTotal` is omitted while any file's length is unknown, and `bytesDone` still counts (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from issue #94). Before, the Hub compressed small files and sent them with no `Content-Length`, so the bytes counted overshot the total;
  - for verification: the file rehash results, the engine's resolved settings, its version and the image digest it ran;
  - for qualification and warm-up: each check's outcome, with a failure's reason code in `CheckOutcome.Reason` and, for the behaviour check, the context it ran at in `CheckOutcome.ContextTokens`; the timings of every request by shape and round, the KV capacity the engine reported at start-up, the served model names, and the measured template overhead;
  - for health: a local health probe every `health.probeInterval`, and from the engine's metrics, the running and waiting requests, the generated-token counter, the KV-cache use, the preemption counter, the prefix-cache queried and hit tokens, the sum and count of the time per output token (HBEP-8, Engine gauges per Instance) and the responses by HTTP status, with `metricsReadAt`, the time of the last successful `/metrics` read. A gauge left out is unknown, never zero (science-agent ruling, 31 Oct 2026, for #87).

  Every time the agent reports, in a report or a health sample, is formatted in UTC, with `Z` (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from issue #94). On the real node the times carried the node's local offset: valid RFC 3339, but misordered by anything that compares the strings.

  A report is a full snapshot, so its size is bounded (science-agent decision, 31 Oct 2026). Both sides refuse a body above 4 MiB. `targetSessions` is at most 1024, and the agent cuts free-text fields short, so a report keeps the timings of all five warm-up rounds within the limit.
- **What it runs.**
  - **The Hub's address.** The lifecycle controller, which reads a checkpoint's file list for its identity (HBEP-3), and the agent, which stages the files, use one Hub base URL, so the identity read and the staging see the same Hub. The controller reads it from `HF_ENDPOINT`, which the launcher sets from its configuration, defaulting to `https://huggingface.co`, and writes it into each Allocation's `agent.json` (HBEP-4, Files first). The agent stages from `agent.json`'s value, and falls back to its own `HF_ENDPOINT` only when the field is absent, since a job submitted over ssh does not inherit the control-plane host's environment. A test or a mirror sets it (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from #146).
  - Staging. It pulls the image by digest into `<stagingDir>/images/`, in the partition's `imageFormat`, which the desired state carries: a SIF file, or a sandbox directory built from the same digest where the partition cannot run a SIF, as on Athena A100 (HBEP-4, open question 15). Both run the same image, so the deployment ID does not change. It downloads missing checkpoint files from the Hub at the full commit id into `<stagingDir>/checkpoints/<repository>/<revision>/`. Each file goes to a temporary name, then is renamed into place, so two Allocations staging at once never see a partial file. An image build's temporary tree under `TMPDIR` is removed as soon as the image is in `<stagingDir>/images/`, so a RAM-disk `TMPDIR` gives its memory back before the engine loads weights; builds happen only on a cache miss. The agent reports the build's peak use of `TMPDIR`. On Helios GH200 a cold build peaked at 55.39 GiB, within one GPU's `memPerGpu`, which fits because the tree is gone before the engine loads (HBEP-4, Submission). There is no check before a build in alpha: a build that exceeds the job's memory ends the job `OUT_OF_MEMORY`, which is an `AllocationFailed` (HBEP-4, Phases), and the agent's last report names the build phase.
  - The worker. It starts it with Apptainer from the staged image, with GPU support, `--no-home`, the checkpoint bound read-only, and a clean environment plus the launch's variables and the cache variables below. The engine listens on a Unix socket in the job's private temporary directory, so no port can clash on a shared node (L-7.7). It runs with `--served-model-name` set to the ModelDeployment's model name, which the front's name rewrite relies on (HBEP-5), and with the Instance's own key in `VLLM_API_KEY`, which the agent sets from the key file; the key is never written in the spec (HBEP-3). It runs with `--max-num-seqs` set to its `launch.maxNumSeqs`, which is always set (What the controller reads). The Instance's serving path is frpc's tunnel, which dials the agent's serving socket, and the agent forwards it to that socket (in beta, behind a router, to the router's; Several workers) (HBEP-6).
  - Home and caches (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from issue #94). Apptainer runs the worker with `--no-home`, so nothing is written to the user's home on the platform; Apptainer binds the home by default, and on the real run the engine's JIT caches wrote there. vLLM's, torch's, FlashInfer's and CUDA's caches, `XDG_CACHE_HOME`, `VLLM_CACHE_ROOT`, `TORCHINDUCTOR_CACHE_DIR`, `TRITON_CACHE_DIR`, `FLASHINFER_WORKSPACE_BASE` and `CUDA_CACHE_PATH`, point at a cache directory in the Allocation's own directory under `stagingDir`, bind-mounted into the container (HBEP-4). Restarts within the Allocation reuse it, and teardown removes it with the Allocation's files (HBEP-4, `RemoveAllocationFiles`). vLLM v0.32.0 otherwise keeps its cache in `~/.cache/vllm`, unless `XDG_CACHE_HOME` or `VLLM_CACHE_ROOT` is set (`vllm/envs.py` lines 338–342 and 711–717). The agent sets these variables itself, outside the serving spec, as the framework sets the listen address, so they are not deployment-ID inputs (HBEP-3). A cache that still writes under `$HOME` finds no such path in the image and can fail the engine's start; that fails the start like any other, and the report carries the engine's log (Risks).
  - The tunnel (HBEP-6). When it is told to run an Instance, the agent checks the staged frpc's SHA-256 and the staged launcher certificate's pin against `agent.json`, writes frpc's configuration, with the Allocation's credential, into the job's private temporary directory with mode 600, and starts frpc. frpc serves one proxy named by the Instance's UID from the agent's serving socket, which the agent forwards to the engine's socket (in beta, behind a router, to the router's socket; Several workers), and logs only to a file in the job's private temporary directory, never to the job's output files, because its log names the session's run ID (HBEP-6, Risks). The agent stops frpc when the Instance ends. In beta, with the bulk path on, `agent.json` carries its settings and the agent also starts the bulk frpc for the Instance, from the same serving socket, restarts it on its own, and stops it with the token frpc (HBEP-6, The bulk path). frpc and the ssh forwards are the agent's transport, not parts of the Instance: the agent restarts them with backoff when they exit, without a controller decision, and a tunnel down is not a dead Instance.
  - Checks. When the desired state asks it to verify, qualify or warm up, the agent runs the checks and the warm-up rounds against the engine's local socket, with synthetic prompts, and reports each outcome. The controller judges the reports: it applies the thresholds and sets the verdicts (Instance start).
  - Sampling parameters of the checks (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from issue #94; amended the same day for reasoning models). Every request of verification, qualification and warm-up sets each sampling parameter it relies on explicitly, so a default the server took from a generation config never changes a check by accident. vLLM takes seven sampling keys from a generation config: `repetition_penalty`, `presence_penalty`, `frequency_penalty`, `temperature`, `top_k`, `top_p` and `min_p` (`vllm/config/model.py` lines 1869–1877; the eighth, `max_new_tokens`, each check sets itself).
    - **A model without a reasoning parser** is checked greedily: `temperature` 0, `top_p` 1, `repetition_penalty` 1, `presence_penalty` 0 and `frequency_penalty` 0, as before.
    - **A model with a reasoning parser** (the serving spec declares `reasoning-parser`) is checked with the staged `generation_config.json`'s own sampling values, set explicitly on every check request: each of the seven keys that the file gives, and the neutral value for each that it does not (`temperature` 1, `top_p` 1, `top_k` 0, `min_p` 0, `repetition_penalty` 1, `presence_penalty` 0, `frequency_penalty` 0; vLLM's own defaults, `vllm/sampling_params.py` lines 305–318). Every such request also carries a fixed `seed`, one per check, so that a repeat of the check draws the same samples. vLLM v0.32.0 takes `seed`, `top_k` and `min_p` on a chat request (`vllm/entrypoints/openai/chat_completion/protocol.py` lines 238 and 274–275) and passes the seed to `SamplingParams` (line 757; `vllm/sampling_params.py` lines 322–323). The verification record holds the values used and the seeds (science-agent decision, 1 Nov 2026, from the second GH200 real-engine run, issue #94).
    - **A reasoning model whose file gives none of the seven keys, or sets `do_sample` false,** or that has no `generation_config.json`, is checked greedily, and the verification record says so.
    - **A value outside vLLM's ranges** in the staged `generation_config.json` fails verification with `VerificationFailed`, naming the key and the value, before any check request is sent, for every model, whether or not its checks sample. vLLM v0.32.0 checks each request against these ranges (`vllm/sampling_params.py`, `_verify_args`, [lines 635–687](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/sampling_params.py#L635-L687)), and it applies the file's values as the server's defaults for any key a request leaves out, so even a greedy check that leaves `top_k` or `min_p` to the server would fail: `presence_penalty` and `frequency_penalty` in [-2, 2], `repetition_penalty` finite and above 0, `temperature` finite in [0, 2], `top_p` in (0, 1], `top_k` an integer of at least -1 (0 and -1 disable it), and `min_p` in [0, 1]. A value that is not a number, or a `top_k` that is not an integer, fails the same way; it is not read as absent. So do a JSON boolean and a `top_k` above 2^63 − 1, which vLLM would take (Python's `bool` is an `int`, and its integers are unbounded): the check is stricter than vLLM there, and its message says so, since both are broken configs. The file is a hashed checkpoint file, so this is the settings contradiction's stop, keyed on the deployment ID alone (Verification), lifted only by a spec change. The value is neither clamped nor replaced by greedy decoding, since either would hide a broken model config (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, for #155).
    - Each check is still one attempt, with no retry.
    - **Why sample a reasoning model:** on the second GH200 run, Qwen3-0.6B failed qualification with `ReasoningBudgetExhausted` under greedy decoding. It spent all 4,224 output tokens reasoning, where the same check had passed on 31 Oct. Its model card says: "For thinking mode, use `Temperature=0.6`, `TopP=0.95`, `TopK=20`, and `MinP=0` (the default setting in `generation_config.json`). **DO NOT use greedy decoding**, as it can lead to performance degradation and endless repetitions." ([Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B, README.md, line 132](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B/blob/main/README.md), accessed 2026-11-01). Its `generation_config.json` gives `temperature` 0.6, `top_k` 20 and `top_p` 0.95 ([generation_config.json](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B/blob/main/generation_config.json), accessed 2026-11-01).
    - **A known limit:** a seed does not make vLLM reproducible. "vLLM does not guarantee the reproducibility of the results by default"; online, only batch invariance does ([`docs/usage/reproducibility.md` at v0.32.0](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/docs/usage/reproducibility.md), accessed 2026-11-01). The corruption check's three requests run one after another on an engine with no other traffic, and need only their first 32 tokens to agree, but on a sampled reasoning model they may still part earlier and fail the check. Such a failure fails the Instance like any other, and `RestartLoop` bounds repeats.
    - **Why:** vLLM v0.32.0 applies them by default. `--generation-config` defaults to `auto`, which loads the generation config from the model's path (`vllm/config/model.py` lines 354–360). Its sampling keys become the server's defaults (`get_diff_sampling_param`, lines 1847–1902), and a request field left unset takes the server's default before OpenAI's (`vllm/entrypoints/openai/chat_completion/protocol.py` lines 715–728). Qwen2.5's generation config adds `repetition_penalty` 1.1, so on the real run the corruption check's greedy requests carried 1.1.
    - **Client traffic** keeps vLLM's default: the framework adds no `--generation-config`. In alpha a serving spec may set it only to `auto`, and may not set `override-generation-config` or `hf-config-path` (below). They stay deployment-ID inputs (HBEP-3), so a later version can allow them.
    - **The generation config the checks read is the one vLLM applies by default** (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, for #169, replacing that day's first reading after the review of PR #176). In alpha the controller refuses, before it provisions and with no Allocation, a serving spec that sets `generation-config` to anything but `auto`, or sets `override-generation-config` or `hf-config-path`, with `Degraded` reason `SpecUnsupported`. Each changes which generation config vLLM applies in a way the checks do not model: `vllm` drops the file's values, a folder or Hub repository is neither staged nor hashed, an override merges on top and its `max_new_tokens` caps every request below what the checks ask for, and `hf-config-path` reads the config from another, separately pinned repository (`vllm/config/model.py`, [lines 354–365, 632–641 and 1812–1902](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/config/model.py#L1812-L1902); `entrypoints/openai/chat_completion/serving.py` lines 177–181). With none of them set, what vLLM applies is the staged `generation_config.json`'s non-null keys: transformers 5.16.1, which vLLM v0.32.0 requires, defaults every `GenerationConfig` field to `None`, so `to_diff_dict` keeps every non-null key the file sets, and vLLM drops nulls (line 1881). So a null in the file is read as absent, as vLLM reads it; every other value that is not a number is refused, as above. `do_sample` false selecting greedy checks is this framework's reading of the author's intent, not vLLM's: vLLM ignores `do_sample`, so client traffic keeps the file's values.
    - **A known limit:** with no `generation_config.json`, vLLM builds its defaults from the model's `config.json` (`vllm/transformers_utils/config.py`, `try_get_generation_config`), which can carry `min_p` and `max_new_tokens`. The checks do not read `config.json`, so such a model is checked greedily, and a broken value there shows as failing checks, bounded by `RestartLoop`.
    - **The verification record** records the model's `generation_config` defaults: the record holds the values of the staged `generation_config.json`, the sampling parameters it sets of those vLLM applies (lines 1869–1877). The file is one of the checkpoint's hashed files (HBEP-3). When a checkpoint has no such file, vLLM falls back to the model's `config.json` (`try_get_generation_config`, `vllm/transformers_utils/config.py` lines 1212–1238), and the record says the file is absent. vLLM v0.32.0 serves the defaults on no endpoint, and its start-up warning (lines 1892–1900) is free text, so neither is read.
  - Restarts and stops. When a part's desired generation rises, it stops the part politely, kills it after `restart.stopGrace`, and starts it again from the same `launch`. It never restarts a part by itself: restarts are the controller's decisions, recorded first.
- **Keys.** The agent generates the Instance's key at Instance start (HBEP-9), passes it to the engine through the environment, and sends it over its channel until the desired state reports it stored (`keyStored`), so a lost report never leaves the front without the key. The controller keeps it in a mode-600 file named by the Instance's UID, which the front reads. The key file holds exactly 64 lowercase hex characters (256 random bits), with no newline, and is written atomically: a temporary file in the same directory, flushed, then renamed into place (science-agent decision, 31 Oct 2026). A reader refuses any other form. A Hub token for a gated checkpoint comes over the same channel. When the download runs in the agent's own process, the token stays in the agent's memory. When a child process downloads, it gets the token through its environment. The token is never on a command line or in a file on the platform (science-agent ruling, 31 Oct 2026, for #87). On the control-plane host it is a mode-600 credentials file (HBEP-11, Credentials; HBEP-3).
- **After the Instance.** The agent stays when its Instance ends, and idles until it is told to stop or the job reaches its hard end. That is what lets a kept Allocation host a later Instance.
- **Unreachable.** An agent that sends no report for `health.agentTimeout` makes its Instance's condition `AgentUnreachable`. The front stops routing new requests to it but keeps counting the ones it has (HBEP-5). The controller restarts nothing on that basis alone (HBEP-6: a path that is down is not a dead Instance). It returns when the reports resume. Its lease still ends on time, and the drain rule still applies.
- **The endpoint,** in the lifecycle controller, has three calls over HTTPS: `POST /agent/v1/register`, `GET /agent/v1/desired` (a long poll from the last generation seen) and `POST /agent/v1/report`. The version is in the path. The agent's own HTTPS connection carries them, never frpc's tunnel (HBEP-6).
  - It listens on loopback on the control-plane host. In ssh mode the agent reaches it through its first forward. In direct mode the front passes `/agent/v1/` through, on the dial-home port, to that loopback endpoint.
  - The launcher creates the endpoint's certificate, and `agent.json` carries its pin (HBEP-11). In direct mode the front's dial-home listener presents the same certificate, so one pin holds on both paths.
  - **Report order** (science-agent rulings, 2 Nov 2026, for issues #220 and #221, settled in PR #228's review). Every report carries `seq`, the agent instance's own count. Its first report is 1, and each later report it builds takes a higher number, fixed when the report is built and kept across its retries; gaps are allowed. The periodic reports and both last-report paths (after a stop instruction, and when the job ends) draw from the one count. The controller then decides, for a registered agent (`NotRegistered` is answered first, as before):
    - **The current instance only.** It keeps a report only from the `agentInstanceID` currently registered for the Allocation: of its registrations, the one with the highest restart count; on a tie, the later registration, then the greater `agentInstanceID` (`regLess`, `internal/lifecycle/backend.go`; #232). A report from any other instance, such as a first execution's late last report after a second execution has registered, is answered as accepted and dropped. It is never answered as not registered, so that agent doesn't register again and look like a third execution. A second execution is still detected at its registration, and fails its Instance.
    - **Newer only.** From that instance, it keeps a report whose `seq` is greater than the held count: the `seq` of the last report it kept. A report refused for any other reason leaves the held count unchanged, so the refused report's retry is kept. An older report, or a resend of the held one (a retry after a lost response), is answered with the usual 204 No Content and dropped: it replaces nothing and refreshes no receipt time. The comparison and the store are one step, under one lock.
    - **Unnumbered, as before.** A report without `seq` (0) comes from an agent built before this rule, which keeps running across a controller upgrade (Compatibility and upgrades). It is kept in arrival order, as before, subject to the current-instance check. An agent that numbers starts at 1, so 0 never stands for a numbered report.
    - **Since the controller's start.** The held counts are in memory, like registrations, and a controller restart clears them. So the newest report stands among those received since the controller started: a report delayed across a restart is answered not registered until the agent registers again, and numbering simply continues.

    So the last report an agent instance built stands, whatever order its reports arrive in. With #220's fix, the last report also waits for a periodic send in flight, within its path's budget: `finalReportTimeout` (30 s) after a stop instruction, and the job-end path's 5 s, which stays below Slurm's `KillWait`.
- **The frps plugin** (HBEP-6, Admission). The lifecycle controller's process also serves frps's server plugin, on loopback at the launcher's port base + 8, never on the agent endpoint's port, which the restricted key opens to every job. It handles `Login`, `NewProxy` and `CloseProxy`, never `Ping`, so a controller restart cuts no established tunnel; while it is down, a reconnecting frpc cannot log in and retries. It admits a tunnel's login by the Allocation's credential, and a proxy only for the UID of an Instance placed on that Allocation. It answers within 1 s from in-memory state, and refuses rather than waiting on the API server, because frps's plugin client has no timeout. The lifecycle controller writes each Instance's `TunnelConnected` condition from frps's proxy list (HBEP-6, Path state).

### Instance start

The Instance phases are HBEP-3's.
- **`pending`** until its Allocation is `Running` and its agent has registered. Registration has its own deadline, `health.registrationTimeout` from the job's start (The allocation agent).
- **`starting`:** the agent stages what is missing, rehashes the checkpoint files present, and starts the worker. An Instance that does not reach `ready` within `health.startTimeout` of its own start instruction fails (Health and restarts). When its `TunnelConnected` condition is present and not True (False or Unknown) at that moment, its reason is `TunnelNotConnected`. When the condition is absent, or True, the reason stays `StartTimeout` (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, for #111). The tunnel gate applies to an Instance's first entry to `ready` only (HBEP-6, Path state). An Instance restarted before it was ever `ready` is still at that first entry: the gate applies, its deadline is `health.startTimeout` from its latest restart, and at that deadline it ends with `TunnelNotConnected` when the condition is present and not True, else the ladder climbs. After a restart at rung 1 or 2 of an Instance that has been `ready`, it returns to `ready` once its checks pass, whatever its `TunnelConnected`, and `health.startTimeout` bounds those checks only. While its tunnel is down it is `ready` but not eligible at the front, the state HBEP-6 gives an Instance whose tunnel drops while it is healthy, and nothing restarts, ends or is released on that basis; the engine's own health signals still drive the ladder (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, for #167). The usual release, replacement and `RestartLoop` counting follow either way. `DialHomeUnreachable` keeps its own scope, a registration that never came (HBEP-4, open question 16). The start instruction is the first desired state that tells the agent to run that Instance, and its time is kept in the Instance's status, so the deadline survives a controller restart. An Instance placed on an older warm Allocation therefore gets the full timeout.
- **`verifying`.** Verification is HBEP-9's alpha checks, the file rehash and the settings comparison:
  - **File rehash:** every checkpoint file present matches its entry in the identity record. A listed file that was never staged, because the engine does not load it, is not a failure (HBEP-3).
  - **Settings comparison:** the engine version, the image digest and `max-model-len` must be reported. The version and the digest must equal the declared ones, and `max-model-len` must equal its declared value when the spec declares one. Every other declared input that the engine reports must equal its declared value, compared by value under one fixed rule (science-agent decision, 31 Oct 2026; PR #91):
    - **What vLLM v0.32.0 reports** is `str()` of each parsed, typed field, after its model hooks (`CacheConfig.metrics_info()`, `vllm/config/cache.py` lines 322–325; the hooks run in `VllmConfig.__post_init__`). So a boolean reads `True` or `False`, a float `4.0`, and some values are rewritten.
    - **The rule.** If both sides read as booleans (`true` or `false`, in any case), they are compared as booleans. Otherwise, if both read as decimal numbers, they are compared as numbers. Otherwise they are compared verbatim, as strings, so an alias such as `fp8` for `fp8_e4m3` is a contradiction, as the deployment ID treats it as another deployment.
    - **A declared `auto`** asks the engine to choose. It is not compared: the reported value is recorded as resolved. For example, `kv-cache-dtype: auto` can report `fp8_e4m3` for a checkpoint whose KV cache is FP8.
    - **`max-model-len`** is compared as an integer. Admission accepts it only as a plain positive decimal integer, refusing vLLM's `32k`, `auto` and `-1` forms, whose meaning only vLLM's parser knows.
    - **A value that a model hook rewrites** (in v0.32.0, `DeepseekV32ForCausalLM` reports a declared `kv-cache-dtype: bfloat16` as `auto`) is a contradiction under this rule, and fails verification with the setting and both values named. The operator then declares the form the engine reports. This is a known limit of alpha. A declared input it does not report is recorded as not reported. A setting it reports that the spec does not declare is recorded (HBEP-3).
  - The engine fingerprint is recorded. HBEP-3 mentions a render check through the template and tokenizer; it is HBEP-9's candidate, and not in alpha.
  - **One record per verification.** Each verification, at start and after every restart that reverifies, writes its own immutable verification record, with the reported settings, the measured template overhead, the model's `generation_config` defaults from the staged file, the sampling values and seeds the checks used, or that they ran greedily (The allocation agent, Checks), the GPU type, the fingerprint, the rehash outcome and the verdict, before the Instance can become ready (HBEP-11). The shape-misfit stop reads the context limit and the overhead from it. It is named `ver-<instance UID>-<generation>`, where the generation counts the Instance's verifications from 1; with several workers, each worker's record carries the worker's name (Several workers). The Instance's status names the latest record. A record that already exists under its name, as after a controller restart, is read and never rewritten.
  - A failed rehash moves the staged copy aside and restarts the Instance at the whole-Instance rung, which stages and rehashes again. A second failure fails the Instance.
  - A settings contradiction fails the Instance at once. Provisioning in that class stops, with the condition `VerificationFailed` and an event, until the serving spec changes (Risks). The stop is keyed on the deployment ID alone, because only a spec change can fix it (science-agent ruling, 31 Oct 2026, for #88).
  - A shape that does not fit (What the controller reads) also fails the Instance at once and stops provisioning in the class, with `VerificationFailed` and an event. This stop is keyed on the deployment ID plus a hash of the resolved shapes. At each reconcile the controller checks the current shapes against the context limit and template overhead that the failed verification record holds. A `workload` edit that makes them fit lifts the stop, with one event (reason `ShapesFit`, proposed in PR #88). An edit to another misfit keeps it stopped, with a new event naming the new shape (science-agent ruling, 31 Oct 2026, for #88).
- **Qualification,** still in `verifying` (open question 12). The agent runs the checks and reports each outcome; the controller judges them. Whatever k is, every Instance passes:
  - health, the engine version, the served model names and the maximum length;
  - one chat request, whose engine fingerprint is recorded;
  - a tool-call smoke test, when `workload.toolCalls` is set;
  - KV capacity from start-up of at least `targetSessions` times `contextTokens.typical` tokens;
  - the corruption check: one request, greedy or, on a reasoning model, sampled with one seed (Checks), sent three times in a row gives three outputs that share their first 32 tokens (every token, if an output is shorter), and none is empty or a single repeated token. Identical text is not required, because vLLM does not guarantee reproducible results by default ([reproducibility](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/usage/reproducibility.html), accessed 2026-10-30);
  - the behaviour check: one request at `contextTokens.longest` with the typical output returns a well-formed answer, and a well-formed tool call when tool calls are on, with no loop. A reasoning model's prompt is shorter (Reasoning models, below).

  A failed check fails the Instance, and its Allocation is released. A failure's reason code is in `CheckOutcome.Reason`, and the controller matches on that code, never on the message.
- **Warm-up,** still in `verifying` (open question 5). The agent runs the rounds and reports their timings; the controller decides when warm-up is done. Synthetic prompts only. Each round sends:
  - one request at each of the three context lengths with the typical output;
  - one at the typical context with the longest output;
  - one tool call, when tool calls are on;
  - then `targetSessions` concurrent requests of the typical shape.

  Warm-up is done at the first round in which every judged shape has settled (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from issue #94):
  - **Judged shapes** have a fixed output length: their requests ask for `min_tokens` equal to `max_tokens` (vLLM's extension; `vllm/entrypoints/openai/chat_completion/protocol.py` line 282 at v0.32.0). They are the three context lengths with the typical output, the typical context with the longest output, and the concurrent requests.
  - **Unjudged shapes** have an output length that varies: only the tool call, which ends with its call. On a model with a reasoning parser the same five shapes are judged, since `min_tokens` fixes the reasoning and the answer together. The tool call still runs in every round, to warm their path, and their timings are reported. Only the settling judgment and the content checks skip them: an HTTP error or a stream cut on any shape, judged or not, still fails warm-up. Whether the tool-call shape answered with a call is qualification's tool-call smoke test, not warm-up's. So the agent reports neither a text answer nor a reply that ends with `finish_reason: length` and no call as a warm-up error. On `main`, `judgeWarmUp` (`internal/lifecycle/judge.go`) fails warm-up on any request that carries an error.
  - **Settled.** A judged shape has settled when its mean total time is at most max(10% of the previous round's, 100 ms) longer than in the previous round. The absolute floor absorbs the noise of a single request: on the real run, requests under 300 ms moved by +22% and +24% between rounds. On long requests the 10% dominates.

  A warm-up that has not settled after five rounds fails qualification. The last round's time per output token and time to first token are recorded as the Instance's throughput at the target sessions. In alpha it gates nothing. In beta the controller then judges it against the profile's expected performance, which with no matching entry records only (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), The throughput gate).
- **Reasoning models** (science-agent ruling, 31 Oct 2026, for #87). These rules hold when the serving spec declares `reasoning-parser`, so the engine splits each reply into reasoning and a final answer:
  - time to first token counts the first delta of either kind, reasoning or content;
  - the tool-call smoke test and the behaviour check judge only the final answer (`content` and `tool_calls`), never the reasoning text;
  - each of those two checks asks for its own answer budget plus 4,096 reasoning tokens (`agentapi.ReasoningHeadroomTokens`). To make room, only the behaviour check's prompt is shortened: to max-model-len − template overhead − the typical output − 4,096, at most `contextTokens.longest` and at least `contextTokens.typical` (`agentapi.BehaviourContextTokens`). The max-model-len is the one the engine reported at verification. The agent reports the context the check actually ran at in `CheckOutcome.ContextTokens`, and the controller records it;
  - a reply that spends the whole budget reasoning, with no final answer, fails the check with reason `ReasoningBudgetExhausted` in `CheckOutcome.Reason`. It is never counted as a wrong answer. The Instance fails like any failed check, and the usual restart and `RestartLoop` limits bound repeats. It does not stop provisioning in the class;
  - warm-up shapes count reasoning tokens as output;
  - the fixed 4,096 is a known limit of alpha. A per-deployment setting comes after alpha.
- **`ready`** once verification, qualification and warm-up pass and its tunnel is connected. In beta a surplus Instance is ranked first, and becomes `ready` only if it outranks a peer (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), The ranking point). The conditions `Verified`, `Qualified`, `WarmedUp` and `TunnelConnected` are true, the latest verification record exists and passed, and the front routes to the Instance once its probe passes (HBEP-5, HBEP-6). `ready` needs `TunnelConnected` True, also while the condition is absent, as HBEP-6 says. #90's reader writes the condition for every placed Instance, so it is absent only until the first read (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, for #111). Only the end reason at `health.startTimeout` depends on whether it is present (`starting`, above).

### Several workers (beta)

An Instance runs several workers behind a router in beta (HBEP-3, HBEP-5). This section is where its mechanism lives, and the other HBEPs point here (science-agent decision, 12 Nov 2026). With one worker, everything below reduces to alpha's behaviour, unchanged.

- **The count.** The ModelDeployment's `spec.workersPerInstance` (a proposed name; default 1) is the number of workers in each Instance. The controller copies it into the Instance's immutable `launch.workers` at creation, and everything about a running Instance reads its own copy: its desired workers, its cap's denominator (HBEP-5) and HBEP-8's inventory.
  - **Each worker is one copy of the Instance's engine launch,** using `tensor-parallel-size` × `pipeline-parallel-size` GPUs. Expert parallelism stays a flag of each worker's engine.
  - **The serving spec's `data-parallel-size` above 1 stays refused** (`SpecUnsupported`): the framework runs its workers itself, so vLLM's own data parallelism isn't used.
  - **The count is capacity, not a deployment-ID input** (HBEP-3), like `instances`. A change applies to Instances created after it: a replacement at handover, or a scale-up. Running Instances keep their count, so a deployment can run mixed counts for a while, which the per-Instance cap handles (HBEP-5).
  - **One Instance per Allocation stays.** Beta adds workers inside an Instance, not Instances beside it.
- **Sizing.** The Allocation's `gpus` is `workersPerInstance` × TP × PP, on one node. It must not exceed the smaller of `gpusPerNode` and the QOS's `maxGpusPerUser`. A larger count is refused on that Provider, never capped (Provisioning), so N is at most 8 on Athena's A100 nodes and 4 on Helios's GH200 nodes. CPUs and memory scale with `gpus`, as today (HBEP-4).
- **Each worker's start**, from the Instance's one immutable `launch`:
  - **its GPUs:** worker *i* gets GPUs *i*·k to (*i*+1)·k − 1 of the job's `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`, in Slurm's order, with k = TP × PP. With several workers, the agent sets that slice as each worker's `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` inside its container, replacing any value the launch's environment sets, and refuses the Instance's start when the job's `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` is unset or names fewer than N × k devices, so two workers can never share a device. With one worker, the launch's own value, if it sets one, wins as in alpha;
  - **its socket:** the launch's command names worker-0's Unix socket, and the agent substitutes worker *i*'s socket (`ContainerEngineSocket`) in that one argument. Nothing else differs between workers;
  - **what's shared:** the job's CPUs, memory and the Allocation's cache directory are shared, not split. That's a stated limit.
- **The router is a part,** named `router`, with its own restart generation, as the agent API already plans (`DesiredInstance.Router`). Its restarts are recorded under that name in `status.restarts`, beside `worker-<i>` and `instance`.
  - **Envoy's listener** is a Unix socket of mode 0600 in the job's private temporary directory (`address.pipe`). frpc keeps dialing the agent's serving socket, as in alpha, and the agent's forwarder targets Envoy's socket instead of worker-0's (HBEP-6). The workers are Envoy's cluster's endpoints, at their sockets.
  - **The agent renders Envoy's configuration** once per Instance generation, with the workers' sockets and the readiness port below. A worker restart re-renders nothing.
  - **The listener's first run** on a pipe is #429's Bytes test, through the whole path (frpc, the agent's forwarder, Envoy, the worker), since S13 ran Envoy alone on TCP. Envoy 1.40.2's `Listener.address` is a `core.v3.Address`, whose oneof carries `Pipe`, and #429's stage 0 validated and ran the pipe listener and endpoints.
- **Readiness.** The agent binds one loopback TCP port at run time (port 0), held for the Allocation's life.
  - Each Envoy endpoint's health check targets it through `health_check_config.address`, with `hostname` set to the worker's name. Envoy 1.40.2 expects that address to be an IP, and `hostname` overrides the check's Host header per endpoint.
  - The agent answers 200 for a verified worker whose engine's `/health` passes, 503 for any other worker, and 404 for an unknown name (HBEP-5, Worker health). "Verified" comes from the desired state: each `DesiredWorker` carries `verifiedGeneration`, the worker restart generation the controller has verified and warmed up, and the agent answers 200 only while it equals the worker's current `Generation`. So a generation raised but not yet verified never takes traffic.
  - **The admin socket** allows exactly `/clusters?format=json`. Envoy's router filter suppresses its own `x-envoy-*` headers toward the workers, and its circuit breakers sit at their field maximum, since the front's cap governs.
  - **The timeouts:** Envoy's route `timeout`, route `idle_timeout` and `stream_idle_timeout` are each the front's maximum `maxRequestDuration`, 2 h, plus 1 h: 10,800 s (HBEP-5). They aren't taken from the deployment's `routing.maxRequestDuration`, which is mutable and can only shorten the front's, since Envoy's configuration is rendered once. A shorter deployment value needs nothing, since the front cuts first. A front whose own maximum is raised above 2 h is a stated limit.
  - **Opt-in:** until spike S2c (#62) sets the default, an Instance gets a router when the controller runs with `--instance-router envoy`. The choice is recorded per Instance at creation, as an immutable `launch.router` outside the deployment ID. An Instance with more than one worker always gets one, so `workersPerInstance` above 1 is refused, with `Degraded` reason `SpecUnsupported` naming the flag, on a controller run without `--instance-router envoy`, since Envoy is staged to the job only with it (#454).
- **Verification, qualification and warm-up are per worker,** each against the worker's own socket, never through the router:
  - **The checks:** with several workers, the desired state asks for checks per worker (`Checks` on each `DesiredWorker`, and `CheckResults` on each `WorkerReport`), so a worker restarted while the Instance is `ready` is verified on its own. Each worker gets the settings comparison and its own verification record.
  - **Durable per-worker state:** the Instance's status gains `workers[]` (proposed), one entry per worker with its name, the restart generation last verified, its verification record and verdict, and whether it's warmed up. The controller computes each `DesiredWorker`'s `verifiedGeneration` from it, so a restarted controller resumes with the workers' state intact. A per-worker restart leaves the Instance's own `Verified`, `Qualified` and `WarmedUp` conditions True. With one worker, the record keeps its name, `ver-<instance UID>-<generation>`. With several, each record carries its worker: `ver-<instance UID>-<worker>-<generation>`, the generation counting that worker's verifications. The record's contents don't change.
  - **The KV and qualification checks:** each worker is checked for ⌈`targetSessions` ÷ N⌉ sessions, so the report's timings stay within its 4 MiB bound at any N. Each exited worker's log tail is at most 8 KiB.
  - **A restarted worker** warms up on its socket while the readiness port still answers 503 for it, so it's never live during warm-up (Notes). Its new process starts only once Envoy holds the worker unhealthy, read from `/clusters`, or at the latest one check interval plus its timeout after the death. Until then Envoy can still route to the worker it last saw healthy, and the new process would take that traffic unverified.
- **Becoming ready.** An Instance enters `ready` the first time when every worker has passed and the router's probe passes, within `health.startTimeout` of its start instruction. The router's probe is the agent's: one `GET /health` through the router's socket, with no `x-session-id`, every report, reported as the router part's health. The front's own probe gates routing only (HBEP-5).
  - It then stays `ready` while the router holds at least one worker healthy, with the front's cap scaled by healthy workers (HBEP-5).
  - The controller writes the router's healthy count to `status.healthyWorkers`, from the agent's report of Envoy's `/clusters`, taking it only from a report whose router part echoes the desired router generation. A worker that fails its active check, or that Envoy has ejected, isn't counted, since Envoy routes to neither.
  - With none healthy it's not eligible, and stays `ready` until the ladder acts.
- **Health and restarts with several workers** (Health and restarts):
  - **A worker's own signal:**
    - its active health check failing in Envoy, its exit, only errors, or no progress, each judged from that worker's report and gauges. Envoy's check counts only for a worker whose current generation is verified: a restarted worker is unhealthy by design during its own warm-up, and `health.startTimeout` from its restart judges it then. An outlier ejection alone isn't a restart signal: a worker that keeps failing requests reaches its rung through only errors.
    - It enters the ladder at the worker rung, for that worker, and the Instance stays `ready` while another worker is healthy. With one worker, a worker restart leaves `ready` for `verifying` as in alpha, router or not.
    - Each worker's rung counts its own `restart.attemptsPerRung` within `restart.window`. A worker that exhausts them takes the Instance to the whole-Instance rung. `status.restarts` keeps up to 32 entries (from 16) for every Instance, so each part's spent rung stays visible. The profile's `attemptsPerRung` keeps its maximum of 8, and a ModelDeployment is refused on a Provider whose profile makes (workers + 2) × `attemptsPerRung` exceed 32, with `Degraded` reason `SpecUnsupported`.
    - A restarted worker must pass within `health.startTimeout` of its restart, or the attempt counts as failed.
  - **The router's signal** (its exit, or the router's probe failing `health.failureThreshold` reports in a row while `/clusters` holds a worker healthy) enters at the router rung:
    - the controller writes `healthyWorkers` 0 first, so the Instance is unroutable;
    - it records part `router`, emits a `failing` event with reason `RouterRestart` (HBEP-8's Reasons table gains it with the code that raises it, #429), and raises the router's generation;
    - the phase stays `ready`, with no re-verify, and `healthyWorkers` returns once `/clusters` holds a worker healthy;
    - after `restart.attemptsPerRung` the ladder climbs to the whole-Instance rung.
  - **The whole-Instance and new-Allocation rungs** are unchanged, and `RestartLoop` still counts Allocations.
- **Metrics:** HBEP-8's inventory names each of the Instance's `launch.workers`, so a worker that reports no gauges shows as unknown rather than absent.
- **Not in beta:** several nodes per Instance, and several Instances per Allocation.

### Health and restarts

- **Malfunction signals,** from the agent's reports, with the profile's `health` thresholds:
  - the health probe fails `failureThreshold` times in a row;
  - only errors: over `errorOnlyWindow`, the engine answered requests and every answer was a server error;
  - no token progress: requests running, and the generated-token counter unchanged for `noProgressTimeout`. A request in prefill generates no token, so in alpha that time-out is 30 min, equal to the front's `idleTimeout`, and a prefill the front allows is never restarted (open question 6). In beta a faster rule also applies once `health.stallTimeout` is set, to an Instance whose launch matches what it was measured under: the counter and the KV usage both unchanged for that long, since a prefill's chunks grow the KV cache (open question 6);
  - a part exits without being told to, whatever its exit code. A clean exit is a failure too (L-2.9);
  - one worker at a full KV cache with requests waiting while another idles, for `kvSkewTimeout`. This needs several workers, so it is not in alpha. Read from each report's snapshot of all workers (science-agent ruling, 13 Nov 2026, for #518):
    - **full:** the worker's KV-cache usage is 0.95 or more;
    - **waiting:** it has a request waiting;
    - **idles:** a sibling the router can route to (warmed up, admitted by the router and held healthy in the latest report) has no request running and none waiting;
    - **not draining:** the worker's generated-token counter is unchanged;
    - **held:** every report for `kvSkewTimeout` shows all of it. A report where it clears, or where either worker's gauge is missing or stale, starts the clock again.

    A few heavy sessions can fill one worker while a sibling idles from load alone, since the router inside an Instance doesn't spill them (HBEP-5, Inside an Instance), but a loaded worker still generates tokens. So the signal is a stuck worker that a sibling could relieve: it acts after `kvSkewTimeout` rather than waiting for `noProgressTimeout`. It enters at that worker's rung, and the Instance stays `ready`.
  - behind Envoy (beta), a worker that Envoy holds unhealthy for `health.failureThreshold` reports in a row, from the agent's report of Envoy's health checks (HBEP-5, Inside an Instance, Worker health).
    - **The ladder:** it enters at the worker rung, for that worker, not the router rung. The Instance stays `ready` while Envoy holds at least one other worker healthy, and only that worker restarts. Its rung-1 verification is what Envoy's check waits for before it takes traffic again: the agent serves each worker's readiness on loopback, 200 only for a verified worker whose engine's `/health` passes, and Envoy health-checks that.
    - **One restart per death:** while a worker's raised restart generation isn't yet applied, the worker raises no further signal, and its exit and Envoy-unhealthy signals count as one restart.

  Plain load never triggers a restart: long queues, high latency, or a full KV cache on the only worker with requests waiting.
- **Crash correlation** (spike S18, #528; science-agent decision, 13 Nov 2026): a worker exit the controller didn't ask for, of a worker that was serving (its Instance `ready` or draining, and the worker verified and warmed up since its last start), within 30 s after such an exit on another Instance of the same ModelDeployment, raises a `failing` event `CrashCorrelated`, regarding the Instance whose exit made the pair and related to the other. It's an event only: each Instance still restarts by its own ladder. In S18 it fired on every poison request that killed two or more Instances, the OpenAI SDK's retries included, and never on a cut tunnel. One Instance's own crash loop isn't counted, since that's the ladder's (`RestartLoop`). Nor is a failed start, a worker that exits before it served: engines that all fail to start on a bad spec are the verification and provisioning loops' to report, not a request's. A draining Instance counts, since it still serves its requests in flight, and a poison request in flight there is then retried to a ready one; the drain's own stop is asked for, so it doesn't count. HBEP-8's Reasons table gains `CrashCorrelated` with the code that raises it.
- **Only fresh gauges act** (science-agent ruling, 31 Oct 2026, for #87). The controller takes no gauge-based action unless the gauges are fresh. Such actions are the idle anchor (through a draining Instance's end, Handover), KV pressure, the only-errors signal and the no-progress signal. Fresh means each of three ages is within two probe intervals (`health.probeInterval`): the read at sampling (the sample's time minus `metricsReadAt`), the sample at the report, and the report since receipt, on the controller's clock (`Health.MetricsFresh`). Stale or missing gauges are unknown, so no such action is taken on them, and a zero running-request count from a stale or missing gauge never feeds the idle anchor. The no-progress signal compares two samples' counters only when `metricsReadAt` advanced between them, so a run of failed reads does not look like a stall.
- **The alpha ladder:**
  1. restart the worker: the settings comparison and warm-up run again, with a new verification record;
  2. restart the whole Instance in its Allocation: stop, rehash, start, verify, qualify and warm up;
  3. a new Allocation: the Instance fails, a replacement Allocation and Instance are created (Provisioning), and the failed Allocation is released at once, since nothing on it serves.

  Once Instances have routers, a signal of the router's enters at the router rung, and a worker's at that worker's rung (Several workers; HBEP-5, open question 2).
- **Attempts.** Each rung is tried `restart.attemptsPerRung` times within `restart.window`, `restart.backoff` apart, before the next rung.
- **Recorded first.** Before each restart, the controller emits an event (type `failing`, the rung as reason, naming the signal and the controller as actor), records the restart in the Instance's `status.restarts`, then raises that part's restart generation on the Instance by compare-and-swap. The agent acts on the generation. The event says that in-flight requests were cut. The attempts per rung are counted from `status.restarts`, so they survive a controller restart.
- **Not routable while restarting.** A restarting Instance leaves `ready` for `verifying`, so the front stops routing new requests to it, and returns after its checks. Behind a router with several workers, a worker restart keeps the Instance `ready` and routable on its other workers, and a router restart keeps it `ready` but unroutable, through `healthyWorkers` 0 (Several workers). With one worker, a worker restart leaves `ready` as in alpha, router or not, and the whole-Instance rung leaves it as before. The front keeps it in its balancer group, and the cut requests leave its count as they fail (HBEP-5).
- **Restart and provisioning loops.** When three Allocations in a row in one class fail before or at rung 3 within `restart.window` (a refused or failed job, a failed check, or a failed whole-Instance restart), the deployment is degraded. Provisioning in that class pauses with backoff, and an event fires. A Rejected submission, a refusal for a full submit limit included, is not a refused job here: it ends no Instance, so it never counts toward `RestartLoop` or the pause (HBEP-4, QOS limits). The count is of failed Instance ends, as on `main`. In beta a hedge loser's end, `HedgeLost`, is not a failed end: like `Drained`, it adds nothing to the class's streak or to `RestartLoop`, and its Instance is not kept for `restart.window` as a failure (`failedEnd`, `internal/lifecycle/health.go`; Hedging).
- **A requeue** fails the Instance, and its Allocation is deleted (HBEP-3, open question 13). The controller sees it from the provider's phase (`Failed`, reason `Requeued`), or from a second execution's registration: another `agentInstanceID`, or a higher `restartCount` (The allocation agent). Nothing from the first execution is reused.

### Handover

- **When the replacement is submitted** (open question 2). For each primary, the replacement Allocation and Instance are created at the primary's drain start minus (the p90 of the Provider's observed queue waits + the class's start-to-ready time + `handoverMargin`), and never before the primary's Allocation is `Running`.
  - The p90 is taken over the last 20 waits in the Provider's status (HBEP-4). With fewer than 20, the largest observed wait is used. With none, the replacement is created as soon as the primary's Allocation is `Running`.
  - The start-to-ready time is the class's last measured time from an Allocation's start to the first `ready` of the Instance created with it, which the ModelDeployment's status keeps. It is 30 min until one is measured.
  - Both inputs live in status, never only in memory (HBEP-11), so a restarted controller plans the same time.
  - On a short queue the replacement is ready about the margin before the drain starts, so the overlap is about the margin, except while the Provider's kept waits hold a long early wait or a pend behind the user's cap (No-overlap mode, the cost). On a long queue the plan reaches back to the primary's start, as in past operation.
  - The replacement carries an annotation naming its predecessor. Each primary has at most one live replacement. In beta that is at most one live replacement hedge, whose members all carry the annotation (Hedging).
- **Both serve.** When the replacement becomes `ready` before the primary's drain starts, both serve, and the front balances across them (open question 4). At the primary's drain start, the replacement becomes the primary, and its own replacement is planned the same way.
- **Lateness.** When the replacement's Allocation has not started by the predecessor's drain start minus the class's start-to-ready time, the deployment gets the condition `ReplacementLate` and an event. With a hedge, that is when no member's Allocation has started. A replacement that starts later cannot be ready before the drain starts. In no-overlap mode the gap is planned, so `ReplacementLate` is not set; `NoOverlapSlot` is (No-overlap mode, below).
- **Same class, same ID.** A replacement in the same accelerator class is a new Instance of the same deployment ID. In another class, it is a new Instance with that class's current ID, and a name pinned to the old ID does not reach it (HBEP-3). The predecessor is released only after the replacement serves.

Drain rule (the same text in HBEP-3, HBEP-5 and HBEP-7): The framework cannot move a lease end, so an Instance starts draining at the latest at its hard end minus the drain window, whether or not its replacement is ready. Make-before-break is the goal: the controller plans the replacement to be ready before that drain start, and the old Instance never drains before its replacement is ready unless that deadline forces it. If the replacement is late, the old Instance still drains on time, capacity drops, and an event is emitted (HBEP-8).

- **Draining.** At its drain start, the hard end minus the Instance's drain window, the Instance becomes `draining`. The window is the profile's `drainWindow`, or the ModelDeployment's `routing.maxRequestDuration` plus the profile's `drainMargin` when that is shorter, as the front computes it (HBEP-5, Draining). The front also enforces this deadline itself, from the hard end copied into `status.leaseEnd` (HBEP-5). It becomes `ended` when the agent's fresh gauges report no running request (Health and restarts: Only fresh gauges act), or at the hard end. A stale or missing gauge is unknown, so it never ends a draining Instance, and never starts the idle count through `endTime` (Retention). The controller then tells the agent to stop the worker and removes the Instance's drain finalizer (HBEP-11). The Allocation follows its retention policy until its hard end.
- **A late replacement** leaves the deployment degraded (open question 10).

**No-overlap mode** (science-agent decision, 1 Nov 2026, within the owner's "gap on Helios" of the same day; Notes). A deployment runs in this mode while no allowed Provider can take the primary's replacement beside it (Provisioning, Where and Per-user limits). On the primary's own Provider, the count leaves no free submit slot, or the two Instances would exceed the QOS's per-user caps on GPUs, CPUs or memory; and no other allowed Provider has a slot. Helios's QOS `now` is the case: its cap of one GPU per user keeps a second Instance from running, though the partition QOS's 2,000 jobs leave a submit slot (HBEP-4, QOS limits). Make-before-break is then impossible, so the drain rule's deadline is what starts the drain. The rule itself is unchanged, and the drain start keeps it as is: the primary drains at its hard end minus its drain window, not earlier, because no replacement can be ready before then. The rule's own clause covers the rest: the replacement is late, the Instance still drains on time, capacity drops, and the drain start's `CapacityDropped` event is emitted (HBEP-8). In this mode:
- the primary serves until its drain start and drains as any Instance does, so no request in flight is cut;
- from its drain start until its successor is `ready`, the deployment has `Degraded` with reason `NoOverlapSlot`, whose message names the Provider and the limit, and no `ReplacementLate`. `NoOverlapSlot` ranks before `SubmitLimitReached` and `CapacityDropped`, so it is the condition's reason during the gap, and the message lists the others that apply (Events and conditions);
- meanwhile the front answers the model's new requests as for a deployment with no ready Instance: 503, `Retry-After: 30`, code `model_inactive`, detail `no_ready_target` (HBEP-5, Errors, the row "Attached, with no eligible target at that level");
- when a submit slot is free and only a per-user cap blocks the overlap, as on Helios, the replacement is created at its planned time as usual (step 8, Handover). It is accepted and pends until the predecessor's job ends and frees its GPU. It holds a place in the shared login's queue meanwhile, and an accepted submission is never read as a start (HBEP-4, Risks). Its pend is behind its own predecessor's per-user cap, not the scheduler's queue, so it records no queue wait. A refused interval (`SubmitLimitReached`) is not part of any wait either, since a wait runs from the recorded job's submission time to its start (HBEP-4, Provider status) and a refusal records no job; a job adopted late after a lost reply (HBEP-4, QOS limits) keeps its own submission time (science-agent rulings, 1 Nov 2026, for #174 and #195). The queued-successor mark decides which Allocations record no wait:
  - **Which Allocations carry it.** The lifecycle controller marks an Allocation in its create request, with the annotation `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/queued-successor` (proposed), when at its creation it is for a no-overlap successor of a primary, or for a retry of one, and that primary's Allocation is on the same Provider and still holds its slot as the slot count reads it (Provisioning, Per-user limits): its phase is not `Ended` with `jobCompleting` false, and its deletion has not completed. The primary is the one the chain replaces, read through any failed successors, not a retry's direct predecessor. The Provider's controller adds no wait for a marked Allocation (HBEP-4, Provider status).
  - **The cases.** The condition covers this step's queued successor. It covers the Allocation created later for a pending successor whose pass was cut short between creating its Instance and creating its Allocation. It covers step 7's retry of a failed successor while the drained primary's job still holds the GPU, as under `Keep` until its hard end. On the one-slot path the successor is created only after the primary's job has left the slot, so by the condition it is not marked, and it records a genuine wait.
  - **Fixed at creation.** The mark stays if the mode lifts while the successor pends, since it still pends behind the same cap. An Allocation created unmarked stays unmarked, even when its Instance is recorded as the primary's no-overlap successor at the drain start. The mark does not pass to the successor's own replacement once the successor is the primary; that one is judged afresh.
  - **What it gives.** The Provider's waits are no longer inflated by queued-path successors' own cap waits.
  - **The cost.** A marked successor adds no sample, but the unmarked samples stay, the first primary's included. So the no-wait rule, planning from the primary's start (Operational readiness 6), applies only while `queueWaits` is empty. Successors no longer refresh the list, so on a Provider used mostly in this mode it rarely fills. Below 20 waits the largest is used (When the replacement is submitted, above), so an early long wait does not age out, and a normal-mode deployment there can still plan from its primary's start. An unmarked wait can still include a pend behind the same user's cap, held by another primary of ours under the same QOS or by the owner's other jobs. A successor's wait after the GPU frees is not added to the Provider's `queueWaits`; the Helios run measures it from the predecessor's job end and the successor's start; **Alpha's fence:** QOS `now`'s one GPU per user makes a second deployment's primary on the Helios Provider pend behind the first, an unmarked pend that, while fewer than 20 waits are kept, could set the plan for a normal-mode deployment there. So in alpha the Helios Provider serves one deployment, and that deployment allows the Helios Provider alone (the alpha runbook, #194) (science-agent ruling, 1 Nov 2026, from PR #205's review).
- when the effective submit limit is 1, so no slot is free, the successor is not submitted early. Step 7 creates its Allocation once the predecessor's job has left the slot: its phase is `Ended` with `jobCompleting` false, or its deletion has completed (Provisioning, Per-user limits). Athena's `plgrid-now` has such a limit, and under the test profile `profiles/athena-now.yaml` it hosts an Instance: alpha's no-overlap run uses it (Graduation criteria);
- the predecessor's Allocation follows its retention policy once its Instance has ended. Under the default `Keep` its job holds the GPU, or the slot, until its hard end, so the gap takes in the rest of the drain window. A deployment in this mode should set `ReleaseWhenIdle` with a short `idleLimit`, and the alpha run on Athena `plgrid-now` does, as beta's run on Helios will (Test plan, on real providers). The controller does not release a kept Allocation early by itself, since that needs approval (open question 8), unless it has `RelayEndedForGood` (Retention);
- the gap is the time from the primary's drain start until its successor is `ready`. On Helios it is about the drain itself, then the idle limit, then the successor's start-to-ready time, plus any queue wait once the GPU frees, which the Helios run measures. On the one-slot path the successor's whole queue wait is added. The ModelDeployment's status keeps the start-to-ready time already; the run on Helios records the gap.

### Hedging (beta)

The science agent's design, 15 Nov 2026, within the owner's decisions on open questions 1, 2 and 8 and on the reserved slot (Provisioning, Per-user limits). Each rule names its source; "proposal" marks the science agent's own. Alpha places one Allocation per need, so none of this runs in alpha.

**The hedge.**
- **What it is attached to** (proposal). A hedge is the Allocations submitted for one need: one missing primary (Reconcile loop, step 7), or one primary's replacement (Handover). Each member is an Instance and its Allocation, created as Provisioning creates any pair, with the current ID of its own Allocation's class. A hedge has at most one live member per Provider.
- **How it is found** (proposal). The first member is created as today. Every later member carries the annotation `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/hedge-group` (proposed), whose value is the first member's name. It is set in the member's create request and never changed. A replacement hedge's members all carry the `predecessor` annotation too. The hedge is the first member and the live Instances that name it. The controller finds them among the Instances it lists by owner (step 3), never by scanning names. An Instance that names no hedge and that no member names is a hedge of one, as in alpha. The Allocations carry nothing new: each is found through its member's `spec.allocationRef`, and keeps the usual `model-deployment` and `provider` labels.
- **One need, one count.** A hedge counts once: as one primary in step 7, and as its predecessor's one live replacement in step 8 (Handover). Today `primaries()` counts every pending Instance, and `replacementOf` returns the first Instance that names the predecessor (`internal/lifecycle/handover.go`, `instances.go`). With hedging each takes the hedge as its unit. Otherwise a two-member hedge for one missing primary reads as two primaries, and step 7's scale-down cancels the newer member on the next pass.

**When a hedge grows.** Open question 2 decides that "further providers are added when the observed waits on the preferred one exceed the time left". Made precise:
- **The planning wait** of a Provider is the statistic Handover plans from: the p90 of the last 20 waits in its status, or the largest while fewer than 20 are kept.
- **The start deadline.** For a replacement, it is the predecessor's drain start minus the class's start-to-ready time: the `ReplacementLate` deadline (Handover, Lateness). A missing primary is wanted serving now. Its deadline is the hedge's creation plus `handoverMargin`, the slack a replacement planned from the waits has between its expected start and its deadline (proposal). **The time left** is the start deadline minus now.
- **A member's expected start** is its job's submission time (`status.atProvider.submitTime`), or now while it has none, plus its Provider's planning wait. A member still `Submitting`, refused for a limit, gains no queue age, so its expected start moves with the clock. **With no wait observed** on the member's Provider, its expected start is the start deadline minus `handoverMargin` for a replacement, and the start deadline itself for a missing primary, so a missing primary's hedge time is its creation plus `handoverMargin` (proposal; revised by the Fable review of PR #602). A replacement with no wait observed is submitted at its predecessor's start (Handover), with the most queue age it can have. A member added then on a faster Provider would serve beside the predecessor for most of the lease, the doubled grant hours that the owner's refinement of 30 Oct removed (open question 2). A missing primary with no wait observed is not predicted late, so its second member waits out the margin rather than going out with the first.
- **A member's hedge time** is:
  - **now,** when its expected start is after the start deadline: the observed waits exceed the time left (open question 2). The controller is predicting its own `ReplacementLate`;
  - **its expected start,** otherwise (proposal). A member still pending then has outlived the prediction, its remaining wait is unknown, and an unknown figure is never read as zero (HBEP-8, Notes), so it exceeds any time left.
  - **but never before 10 minutes after its submission** (`hedgeMinWait`, proposal; science-agent ruling on #627, 17 Nov 2026), in the second case: the hedge time is at least the member's submission time, or now while it has none, plus 10 minutes. On a Provider whose observed waits are all near zero, the planning wait is near zero, so without it a member still pending one pass after its submission would have outlived its prediction and gain a sibling at once, and on two fast Providers both would start, giving a started loser: the insurance that A rescue, not insurance rules out. A member that is merely starting normally isn't late. Ten minutes is under the default `handoverMargin`, so a missing primary still gets its second member within its margin. A fixed value in beta; it becomes a profile setting only if beta's waits show a platform needs another. The first case, a member predicted late, is unchanged.
- **The rule.** While every member is still pending, the hedge gets its next member once every member has reached its hedge time.
- **Worked by hand,** with a 30 min margin and start-to-ready time:
  - a replacement planned from its waits is submitted at the drain start minus (wait + 1 h). Its expected start is the deadline minus the margin, so it gets no member at submission, and one at that expected start if it still pends;
  - a replacement whose plan is clamped at the predecessor's start, a long queue, gets its member at once whenever its expected start is after the deadline;
  - a missing primary whose Provider's planning wait exceeds the margin gets its member in its first pass, as the user story of `example-27b` has it. With no wait observed its expected start is its deadline, so its second member comes at its creation plus the margin, and only if the first still pends. Taking a slot on each Provider from the start, in the first case, is accepted: a pending member costs no grant hours, and reserving slots to protect capacity is within the owner's word of 15 Nov 2026 ("Can always reserve the slots without my permission").
- **A rescue, not insurance.** A member added at the first member's expected start is on time only when its own planning wait plus start-to-ready time fits in the margin. Otherwise the replacement is late as it would have been: the drain rule applies, and the hedge shortens the gap after the drain. Insuring the tail on time would mean submitting on the second Provider before the first member's expected start while that start is still on time, which on the normal path gives started losers. This design submits a second member early only when the first is predicted late.
- **Which Provider is next.** Provisioning's order, skipping the Providers already in the hedge: for a replacement, the predecessor's Provider first, then the allowed order (What the controller reads); for a missing primary, the allowed order. The Provider must pass a new primary's checks (Provisioning, Where): `Ready` and batch; its partition's class has a current ID, neither stopped nor paused (Instance start; Restart and provisioning loops); its time limit can host an Instance (A partition too short); its sizing fits; and a slot is left (Interactions, below). Also, as for a replacement beside its predecessor, the member and our Allocations on that Provider that have not ended must fit its QOS's per-user caps on GPUs, CPUs and memory (Per-user limits), so no member pends behind a cap of our own, as a second Instance would under Helios's QOS `now` (proposal).
- **A Provider passed over** is tried again at the next pass. It sets no `Degraded` reason, since a hedge is extra capacity (proposal). The first member's conditions, `SubmitLimitReached` and the others, are set as today. The search does not go through the code's `noProvider()`, which explains a missing primary.
- **No member is added** while any member's job has started and its Instance has not ended: that member is expected to serve within the class's start-to-ready time.
- **Restarts.** Each pending member's hedge time joins step 11's deadlines. Every input is in status (the submission time, `queueWaits`, the start-to-ready time), so a restarted controller adds the same members at the same times.

**The width.**
- **The maximum** is the ModelDeployment's `hedgeWidth`, 2 by default (What the controller reads; proposal). A lower value applies to members added after it and cancels none, so lowering it releases nothing.
- **Slots and per-user limits** bound it further: a member is added only where a slot is left and the caps allow (Interactions).
- **The start-up failure rate** (open question 1: it "sets the hedge width").
  - **Measured.** Each Allocation of the deployment whose job started adds one start-up outcome to its class: a success when its first Instance becomes `ready`, and a failure when that Instance ends before `ready` because its start, verification, qualification or warm-up failed, its registration or start timed out, or its job was requeued, failed or lost. A failed verification counts when it is a rehash failure, a staging or node fault. A settings contradiction and a shape misfit add no outcome: they also fail verification, but they fault the spec and stop the class (Instance start). Nor does an Instance ended before `ready` by a hedge loss at the win, a scale-down, a shutdown or a deletion. An Instance that passes its checks and is ranked out before `ready`, a hedge loser's included, adds a success (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), How k adjusts).
  - **Kept.** The ModelDeployment's status keeps the last 20 outcomes of each class, beside the class's start-to-ready time: `startOutcomes` (proposed; an amendment to HBEP-11's API, like the spec fields above). Each outcome holds its Allocation's UID, which counts it once, with its result and its reason. A class's rate is its failures over its outcomes, shown as "failures of count" from the first outcome, and unknown only with none; the last 20 bound the list's size, and a full window isn't required. A platform's rate, which beta's criterion reports, pools the outcomes of every ModelDeployment's classes on that platform's partitions. They live in the ModelDeployment's status, not the Provider's, because the lifecycle controller owns only `DialHomeUnreachable` there (HBEP-4, Provider status, Writers; HBEP-11, Field managers), and because a failed start is often the deployment's own, as out-of-memory at start was (open question 1, Why).
  - **Used.** Beta measures and shows the rate, and the width stays `hedgeWidth`. How the rate sets the width is decided from beta's measurement (open question 1). Lean: count a member's planning wait as the wait divided by (1 minus its class's rate), since a failed start costs a new submission with no queue age, so a class that often fails to start is hedged earlier.

**The winner and the losers.**
- **The winner** is the member whose Instance becomes `ready` first (open question 1): the earliest `status.readyTime`, then the older member by creation time, then by name. The pass that sees it `ready` resolves the hedge (step 6). In a replacement hedge the winner is the replacement: both serve, and at the predecessor's drain start the winner becomes the primary (Handover, Both serve).
- **Its ID.** Each member's Instance was created with its own Allocation's class's current ID (Provisioning, The Instance; HBEP-3, Current IDs). So in a hedge across classes the winner already carries its class's current ID, and nothing is rewritten. In a replacement hedge, a winner in another class is a new Instance with that class's ID, which a name pinned to the predecessor's ID does not reach (Handover, Same class, same ID).
- **Pending losers are cancelled** (open question 1), with no approval (open question 8; HBEP-10, What needs approval). In the same pass, every member whose job has not started ends its Instance with reason `HedgeLost` (proposed), and its Allocation is deleted. Its finalizer cancels the job (Release), and its release cause names the hedge and the winner (Release, The release cause). A member still `Submitting` or `SubmissionUnknown` counts as pending, and its deletion cancels whatever carries its tag (open question 11, Deletion). In `internal/lifecycle` this is `end` with `release` true.
- **Started losers are kept** (open questions 1, 12 and 14; the owner's concern of 14 Nov 2026). A member whose job has started when the winner serves:
  - **its Instance ends** with reason `HedgeLost`. One that is already `ready` drains first, as a surplus Instance does (Release, scale-down). With over-allocation on, it ends at its ranking point instead (the last item below);
  - **its Allocation is not released.** `HedgeLost` is no failure, so, like `Drained`, it must be left out of `releaseRequired` (`internal/lifecycle/instances.go`). That function releases the Allocation of an Instance ended for any other reason at once, which would be the eager release the owner's concern rules out. The end is `end` with `release` false, and `anchorIfLast` writes `idle-since`;
  - **its end is no failed end** (revised by the Fable review of PR #602). `HedgeLost` must be left out of `failedEnd` (`internal/lifecycle/health.go`) too, which counts every end but a drain. Otherwise each started loser adds to its class's failure streak, three in `restart.window` set `RestartLoop` and pause provisioning in that class, and the loser's Instance is kept as a failure's tombstone (`instances.go`) though nothing failed (Health and restarts, Restart and provisioning loops);
  - **it's kept as a warm spare** (owner decision, 15 Nov 2026, asked in the science agent's terminal: "Keep it, as warm spare"). It isn't released to save grant hours, though it may idle until reused or until its policy ends it: the owner judged the capacity worth more than the hours, since getting it back can take days.
  - **its policy then applies** (Retention). Under the default `Keep`, it stays until its keep date, the profile's `maxIdle` (Athena's 30 min, a site rule that still applies) or its hard end. Under `ReleaseWhenIdle`, the controller releases it after its idle limit with no approval, since an Allocation emptied under the agreed spec is HBEP-10's row that needs none (HBEP-10, What needs approval). A release before then, outside the policy, is a `Release` Operation and needs approval (open question 8);
  - **it is warm capacity** of the deployment (open question 14). The deployment's next missing primary is placed on it before any new submission, and its next replacement after the predecessor's own Provider, in the order Provisioning, Where gives (proposal). So a winner that fails is replaced with no queue wait. A replacement goes there only when the warm Allocation's drain start is later than its predecessor's, or it would drain with it (proposal);
  - **once question 12's ranking is built,** a started loser is instead an over-allocation candidate: its Instance runs its checks and is ranked, by that question's design. Until then it is one of that question's "rest", kept as warm capacity per its policy. Beta builds it: with `overAllocation` 1 and no surplus or warm Allocation of the deployment on the loser's Provider, the loser isn't ended at the win, runs its checks and is ranked against the winner alone; otherwise it ends at the win, as above (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), Hedging's started losers).
- **A failed member** leaves the hedge as any failed Instance does: its Allocation is released (Release), and it counts toward `RestartLoop` in its class. The hedge goes on with its other members. With none left, the need is planned again, as any missing primary or replacement.

**Interactions.**
- **The reserved slot** (Provisioning, Per-user limits; owner, 15 Nov 2026).
  - Every member counts on its own Provider from its creation, like any Allocation.
  - In a replacement hedge, the member on the predecessor's Provider takes the predecessor's reserved slot. A member on another Provider takes only a slot left beyond that Provider's reserved slots, as a new primary does. So a hedge never takes the slot another serving primary holds for its replacement, which is the reserved slot's reason. A missing primary's members do the same on every Provider.
  - The predecessor's reservation lapses once its hedge has a member, since it then has a live replacement.
  - A pending loser's slot returns when its deletion completes, with `jobCompleting` false. A started loser keeps its slot while it is kept, as any Allocation that has not ended.
  - **The winner's own slot** (proposal, in Per-user limits' definition of who holds a reserved slot). A winner on a Provider other than its predecessor's holds a reserved slot there from its win, when the controller would create its replacement (Handover: the deployment has no end time, or one after the winner's drain start). Without it, another deployment's primary could take that Provider's last slot while the winner serves beside its predecessor, and the winner's replacement would find none.
  - In host-dialed mode a Provider has at most 2 Allocations (HBEP-6), and a serving primary with its reserved slot fills its own. So a replacement hedge's second member lands only on another Provider with a slot beyond its reservations, in practice one with nothing of ours serving.
- **Slots reserved atomically** (Provisioning). Each member reserves its slot under its own Provider's lock before its Instance is created. A pass takes one Provider's lock at a time, as the lock order in `internal/lifecycle/slots.go` requires, so a hedge never holds two. A member whose reservation fails is not added, and the hedge keeps what it has. A pass cut short between a member's Instance and its Allocation is finished by the next pass under the same name (`ensureAllocation`). The hedge annotation was written in the Instance's create request, so the next pass reads the same hedge. Two members on one Provider, which only a leader hand-over race can make, are one too many. The older, by creation time and then UID, is that Provider's member, and the newer waits, as the newer of two racing replacements does (Per-user limits). It is never cancelled for the race: a member of a hedge with no winner is no hedge loser, and cancelling it would lose its queue age. It stays in the hedge and is resolved with it, as a winner or a loser like any other member (revised by the Fable review of PR #602).
- **No-overlap mode** (Handover). The mode holds while no allowed Provider can take the primary's replacement beside it, so while it holds no Provider passes a hedge's checks, and the successor is the hedge's only member, as today. If another Provider gains room during the gap, the successor's start deadline has passed, so a member is added there at once. That member is never marked as a queued successor, since the mark needs the primary's Allocation on the member's own Provider (No-overlap mode, Which Allocations carry it). Its pend is in that Provider's queue, and it records a genuine wait. The gap ends, and `NoOverlapSlot` clears, when the hedge's winner is `ready`.
- **Operations** (HBEP-10).
  - **A scale-down while a hedge has no winner,** by step 7 or by a `Scale` Operation's plan, which runs step 7's selection: the hedge counts as one primary, ranked by its most advanced member (pending while no member's job has started, else starting) and dated by its first member's creation, and it goes whole. Each member ends by its own state: its Instance ends, an Allocation whose job has not started is cancelled, and one whose job has started follows its retention policy, as `removeSurplus` does today. A surplus primary's replacement hedge goes with it, never promoted (step 7).
  - **Approval.** The plan lists each member as its own target, with its own effect and cost (HBEP-10, The release cost). A member of a hedge with no winner is not a hedge loser, since no Instance has served. So a pending member's effect is "Cancel a pending Allocation, other than a hedge loser", which needs approval by HBEP-10's lean, and the plan shows its queue age (HBEP-10, What needs approval; open question 2). A started member's effect is its Instance's end with its Allocation left to its retention policy. When that policy releases it before its hard end (`ReleaseWhenIdle`, a keep date before the hard end, or the profile's `maxIdle`), it is HBEP-10's row for a drain that retention turns into an early release, and needs approval; kept until its hard end, it needs none (open question 8).
  - **A stale plan.** A hedge that grows or resolves between a plan and its commit changes the plan's targets, so the plan's ID changes and the Operation turns `Stale` (Reconcile loop, Operations). While an Operation is `Executing`, no member is added to a hedge among its committed targets (proposal).
- **Queue waits.** A cancelled loser never started, so it adds no wait (HBEP-4, Provider status). A Provider whose members keep losing keeps its older waits, and a queue that has slowed shows only once a member there starts. A member's hedge time still comes at its expected start, so the next member is added when it pends past that.

**Events and conditions.**
- The winner raises the usual `ready` event, and at its predecessor's drain start the usual `replaced` with `HandedOver`.
- Each loser raises `replaced` with reason `HedgeLost` (proposed), regarding the loser's Instance, with the winner in `related`. The reason joins HBEP-8's Reasons table with the code that raises it, since the table is checked against the code. It needs no new type (HBEP-8, Six types).
- `Progressing` is true with reason `Provisioning` while a hedge has no winner, and its message names the hedge's Providers. `ReplacementLate` is set when no member's job has started by the start deadline (Handover, Lateness). A hedge adds no `Degraded` reason, and the ranking of `Degraded` reasons is unchanged.

**Prior art.** Kueue MultiKueue's "keep the first admitted, withdraw the rest; add targets in rounds" and GlideinWMS's pilot demand law (Prior art and reuse) are the precedents. New here: the first to serve wins, members are added from observed waits against the deadline, and a started loser is kept.

### Over-allocation and qualification (beta)

The science agent's design, 16 Nov 2026, within the owner's decisions on open questions 8, 12, 13 and 14, on the reserved slot of 15 Nov 2026 and on the warm spare of the same day. Each rule names its source; "proposal" marks the science agent's own. Alpha runs k = 0 and its throughput gates nothing, so none of this runs in alpha. HBEP-4 holds the profile's side (Platform profile schema, Expected performance).

**The throughput gate.**
- **What it judges** (open question 12; owner, 29 Oct 2026: "throughput at the target sessions against absolute gates from the platform profile, such as ms per token and time to first token"). The Instance's throughput is what alpha already records: the settled warm-up round's mean time per output token and mean time to first token over its concurrent requests, `targetSessions` of them at `contextTokens.typical` and `outputTokens.typical` (Instance start, Warm-up; `throughput` in `internal/lifecycle/judge.go`). With several workers, each worker's own measurement, at ⌈`targetSessions` ÷ N⌉ sessions, is judged, so one slow GPU isn't hidden in the Instance's mean (proposal; Several workers).
- **Its reference** is the platform profile's `expectedPerformance` entry that matches the Instance: its deployment ID, its declared capacity flags with `max-num-seqs` from `launch.maxNumSeqs`, and its concurrent shape per worker (HBEP-4, Platform profile schema). The ID carries the class, the checkpoint, the engine version and the image digest (HBEP-3, Deployment ID), so an entry holds for one class and one engine build.
- **The limits** (proposal). With the entry's tolerance of t percent, 100 by default, a measurement passes when its time per output token is at most the entry's × (100 + t) ÷ 100, and its time to first token at most the larger of the entry's × (100 + t) ÷ 100 and the entry's plus 100 ms. Time to first token is one interval per request, so it gets warm-up's absolute floor (Instance start, Warm-up); time per output token is a mean over every token, so it needs none. The gate is for broken nodes, and ranking (below) drops slow ones (HBEP-3, Qualification). So the tolerance errs wide: a false fail releases a good node, which can take days to get back, while a false pass keeps a slow one that ranking or its next handover drops.
- **A miss is measured twice** (proposal). A first miss restarts the worker at rung 1, whose warm-up measures again (Health and restarts, The alpha ladder), recorded first like any restart. A second miss fails the Instance with the existing `QualificationFailed`, whose message names both measurements, the limits and the entry's `date`. As for any failed check, its Allocation is released at once (Instance start, Qualification; open question 8). The gate judges an Instance's first qualification and that retry only. A later restart's warm-up records its throughput and gates nothing, so a restart never releases an Allocation for throughput.
- **No reference, no gate** (open question 12: "a reference must not come from the Instances it judges"; proposal for the cases). The gate records only, as in alpha, when no entry matches, when the matching entry is 90 days or more past its `date`, or when the entry is suspended (below). The `Qualified` condition's message says which. A gate that records only never fails an Instance.
- **The gate's record** (science-agent ruling on #616's plan, Q1, Q2). Each Instance keeps its gate's verdicts in `status.gate` (proposed; additive, HBEP-11): each measurement with its time and value, so a candidate waiting at its ranking point isn't gated again after a part exits, and the failure message names the first measurement. The retry restart after a first miss is forced at the worker rung and counts toward no `RestartLoop`, so a gate miss never climbs the ladder into a release.
- **`ReferenceSuspect` is emitted once,** when the suspension is written, since `status.suspendedReferences` is the level truth and no condition carries it (science-agent ruling on #616's plan, Q11).
- **At most two releases for one reference** (science-agent ruling on #616's plan, Q7). A pass counts the second misses it has already failed against an entry, so three Instances retrying in one pass release at most two Allocations, and the entry is then suspended, as Risks states.
- **A suspect reference** (proposal; revised by the Fable review of PR #612). When the two most recent `Passed` or `Missed` verdicts against one entry, from two Allocations, are both `Missed`, the entry is suspended for the deployment. **An entry is one profile's** (science-agent ruling on #652, 16 Nov 2026): it is keyed by the PlatformProfile it comes from, its key and its `date`. Two profiles' entries for one class are two entries, so a right entry's passes never reset a wrong entry's misses, and several Providers sharing one profile share its entries, so together they release at most two. **Every gated Instance counts,** not only an Allocation's first: an Instance placed on a warm Allocation is judged by the gate, and its verdict is recorded durably against the entry in the pass that judges it, before anything else that pass might return on, so a miss is never lost. **The record** is `status.referenceMisses` (proposed; additive, HBEP-11's API), at most 32 items: one per entry whose latest verdict is `Missed` and that isn't suspended yet, holding the profile, the entry's key and `date`, and the Instance's and Allocation's UIDs and time. A `Passed` verdict against the entry removes it; a `Missed` from another Allocation suspends the entry and removes it. It's kept in the ModelDeployment's status, so it outlasts a queue wait longer than `restart.window`, which prunes counts derived from Instances, and a controller restart. When the entry is suspended, a `degraded` event with reason `ReferenceSuspect` (proposed) names the entry. The suspension is held in the ModelDeployment's status, not derived from the kept outcomes: `suspendedReferences` (proposed; an amendment to HBEP-11's API, as `startOutcomes` is) lists each suspended entry's profile, key and `date`, written in the pass that records the second miss. The gate records only against an entry listed there with the same `date`, and each such outcome records `Suspended`. The controller removes the listing when the profile's matching entry has another `date` or is gone. So the suspension outlives the class's 20 kept outcomes, and a restarted controller reads it from status. Two failures stop short of `RestartLoop`'s three (Health and restarts, Restart and provisioning loops), so a wrong reference costs at most two Allocations, never the class. The two lists' caps err toward recording only (science-agent ruling on #652): a miss that the full `referenceMisses` can't hold suspends its entry rather than evict another's, and while `suspendedReferences` is full the gate records only for the deployment, with one `ReferenceSuspect` event when it fills, and no listing is dropped to make room. It is Cluster API's MachineHealthCheck short-circuit applied to a reference (Prior art and reuse).

**k, and when the extra is submitted.**
- **k** is the ModelDeployment's `overAllocation` (integer, optional; beta, proposed; an amendment to HBEP-11's API, like `hedgeWidth`): the extra Allocations per Provider, 1 by default (owner, 29 Oct 2026: "start with k = 1 per provider"). 0 turns over-allocation off. Beta admits 0 and 1 only, since no rule adjusts k yet (How k adjusts, below).
- **When** (proposal). Step 7 creates the extra in the pass that creates new Allocations for missing primaries on a Provider, after every missing primary it can place there: for a new deployment, a scale-up, or primaries lost to failure that no warm Allocation could take. One extra goes to each such Provider, with an Instance created with it as Provisioning creates any pair. A missing primary placed on a warm Allocation creates no new Allocation, so it brings no extra. Warm Allocations count toward k only here, in the extra's creation bound: no extra is created on a Provider while the deployment's warm Allocations and surplus Instances there already reach k, since a warm spare already covers what an extra would. A hedge's later members bring none, since a hedge already spreads one need over Providers (Hedging). Planned replacements bring none in beta (open question 12). A pass cut short before the extra leaves its round without one.
- **Its checks.** The Provider has just passed a new primary's checks (Provisioning, Where). Also (proposal):
  - **the bound:** the deployment holds no surplus there (How it counts, below), and no Allocation there with no Instance (warm). So k bounds the deployment's surplus Allocations per Provider at any time, whatever their phase;
  - **a slot beyond its own handovers:** the Provider's count (Provisioning, Per-user limits: listed plus reserved), plus one for each of the deployment's primaries there that holds no reservation yet but will once `Running`, leaves a slot for the extra. A primary will hold one when the controller would create its replacement: the deployment has no end time, or one after that primary's expected drain start, its creation plus its time limit minus its drain window. Without this, an extra created beside a pending primary takes the slot that primary reserves once it runs, and its handover finds none (Per-user limits, Why);
  - **a pending extra yields its slot** (science-agent ruling on #616's PR 4, 17 Nov 2026): when a missing primary, of any deployment, is refused on a Provider only for its submit limit, the controller cancels the newest pending extra there, so the primary submits in that slot. An extra is insurance, and a primary that can't be submitted at all is serving capacity short; a pending extra costs only its queue age, and cancelling it is over-allocation's own act, with no approval (HBEP-10, Notes). A started extra is never cancelled for this. The extra isn't created again on that Provider while its count leaves no slot, since the slot check above applies to each new extra;
  - **the per-user caps:** the extra and our Allocations there that have not ended, of every deployment, fit the QOS's caps on GPUs, CPUs and memory, so no extra pends behind a cap of our own, as no hedge member does (Hedging, Which Provider is next).
- **In host-dialed mode** a Provider has at most 2 Allocations (HBEP-6), and a primary with its reservation fills them. So an extra fits there only for a deployment whose end time comes before its primary's drain start, which reserves no slot. A deployment that hands over gets no extra on such a Provider: k is 0 there in effect, a stated limit until HBEP-4's open question 17 decides a third Allocation (open question 12).
- **No-overlap mode** admits no extra. The mode holds while no allowed Provider can take a second Allocation beside the primary, and the extra's slot and caps checks fail by the same counts (Handover, No-overlap mode).

**How it counts** (proposal; with `overAllocation` 1; revised by the Fable review of PR #612). Step 6, step 7, step 8 and the reserved slot take as the deployment's primaries the `spec.instances` most advanced of its Instances that are not draining or ended and have no predecessor still counted. The order: one that is `ready` first, with one that also has a live replacement before one that hasn't, since its handover has begun; then one that isn't `ready` but has a live replacement; then one that has passed its checks and is not yet `ready`; then one whose job has started; then a pending one. Among equals: of `ready` ones, the later hard end first, so that a scale-down drains the nearest hard end, as today; of the others, the earlier job start, then the older by creation time, then by UID. A hedge counts once, by its most advanced member (Hedging). These are the chosen primaries, and the rest are the deployment's surplus. A hedge's started loser kept as a candidate (Hedging's started losers, below) is surplus on its Provider too, whether its hedge is a missing primary's or a replacement's.
- **What step 7 takes, in order,** which are also a `Scale` plan's targets (HBEP-10, The plan):
  1. every `ready` surplus Instance, which drains, the nearest hard end first. A surplus Instance becomes `ready` only by outranking a peer. The peer's drain starts once the new Instance has been `ready` for the front's probe interval, so the front routes to it before the peer stops taking requests (science-agent ruling on #616's plan, Q5); meanwhile both are `ready`, and the outranking mark keeps step 7 from draining the new one. **The mark** is the annotation `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/ranked-out` (proposed) on the peer, naming the new Instance, written before the new Instance becomes `ready`. A marked peer is surplus once its named Instance is `ready`, whatever their hard ends; step 6 clears the mark if the named Instance stops being passed (restarted, failed or ended), so a peer never drains without a `ready` successor, so this happens only when `spec.instances` is lowered;
  2. surplus that isn't `ready`, beyond k per Provider, counting surplus Instances only, never the deployment's warm Allocations: pending ones first, cancelled, newest first; then started ones, whose Instance ends and whose Allocation follows its retention policy, as `removeSurplus` does for a started hedge member (Hedging, Interactions). That happens when `spec.instances` or `overAllocation` is lowered.
- **Left alone:** up to k surplus Instances per Provider that aren't `ready`, counting surplus Instances only. A warm Allocation never counts toward k here, so a drained Instance's Allocation turning warm never makes a later pass cancel a pending surplus that the lowering left alone: that cancel would lose queue age outside any plan's approval (science-agent ruling on #635, 16 Nov 2026). A pending one keeps its queue age, and a started one reaches its ranking point (below).
- **What follows.** Before either Instance of a round has started, the older by creation time, then by UID, is chosen, and nothing yet depends on which. When the round's primary fails, its extra is chosen in its place, and no Allocation is made. An extra that passes its checks first is chosen and becomes `ready`, and the round's primary, still in its checks or pending, becomes the surplus. If that primary already has a live replacement, it stays chosen, and the extra waits at its ranking point (below).
- **A surplus Instance** holds no reserved slot and gets no replacement.
- **A replacement waits for no ranking** (a stated limit; revised by the Fable review of PR #612). A chosen primary's replacement is created at its planned time (Handover), whatever its round's state. When it comes due before the round resolves, the round ends with no ranking: the primary now has a live replacement, so it stays chosen and is no peer (below), and the extra, when it passes, has no peer and stays a warm spare. It was still judged by the throughput gate, which is what catches a bad node. So ranking rarely runs where a primary's replacement is due at its start, with no wait observed or on a long queue (Handover, When the replacement is submitted). It runs where the replacement is planned later, on a short queue, and for a deployment whose end time comes before its primary's drain start.

**The ranking point** (open question 12; owner, 29 Oct 2026: "Rank by throughput at the target sessions"; proposal for the rest).
- **When** (revised by the Fable review of PR #612). In the pass that sees an Instance pass qualification, warm-up and the gate, the selection is made with it as passed (How it counts). If it is chosen, it becomes `ready` as today, so ranking never holds back an Instance the deployment needs. If it is surplus, it becomes `ready` only by outranking at its ranking point, never as today. It is ranked once every chosen primary on its Provider is `ready` or has ended. Until then it waits in `verifying`, its checks done, and `health.startTimeout` no longer judges it.
- **Its peers** are the deployment's primaries on the same Provider, so of one class (one partition per Provider; HBEP-4, open question 12), that are `ready`, not draining and have no live replacement. A primary with a live replacement is in its handover, which ranking leaves alone. Ranking compares one class's measurements taken by one method, so it needs no reference: a class with no entry still ranks. A hedge's started loser is the one exception: its one peer is its hedge's winner, whatever their classes, and never the deployment's other primaries on its Provider (Hedging's started losers, below).
- **The order.** A lower time per output token ranks higher. The candidate is compared with its worst peer: the highest time per output token, then the nearest hard end, then by name. It outranks that peer only when its time is lower by more than 10% of the peer's, warm-up's settling tolerance (Instance start, Warm-up), so a serving peer is kept against noise.
- **It outranks:** it becomes `ready`, and in the same pass the peer drains, as a surplus Instance does (Release, scale-down), with no request cut, and ends with reason `RankedOut` (proposed).
- **It does not, or it has no peer:** it ends with `RankedOut` without ever being `ready`; a hedge's started loser ends with `HedgeLost` instead (Hedging's started losers, below).
- **`RankedOut` is no failure.** Like `Drained` and `HedgeLost`, it must be left out of `releaseRequired` (`internal/lifecycle/instances.go`) and `failedEnd` (`internal/lifecycle/health.go`). The end is `end` with `release` false, so `anchorIfLast` writes `idle-since` (Hedging, Started losers are kept).

**The lowest-ranked is kept** (open question 12: "The rest are released, or kept as warm capacity, per the retention policy"; open question 13, keep by default; the owner's concern of 14 Nov 2026 and the warm spare of 15 Nov 2026).
- **Ranking never releases an Allocation.** The ranked-out one is warm capacity of the deployment (open question 14). Its next missing primary is placed on it before any new submission, and a replacement goes there when its drain start is later than its predecessor's (Provisioning, Where; Hedging, Started losers are kept). The new Instance is qualified again, the gate included. A stated limit: a ranked-out Allocation that started later than a primary whose replacement is already submitted doesn't take that replacement over, since warm capacity is placed on only when a replacement is created (Provisioning, Where).
- **Its policy releases it.** Under `Keep`: at its keep date or the deployment's end time, at the profile's `maxIdle` (Athena's 30 min, a site rule), or at its hard end. Under `ReleaseWhenIdle`: after its idle limit. Each is the controller's own release of an Allocation emptied under the agreed spec, which needs no approval (HBEP-10, What needs approval). A deletion releases it at once, as `RelayEndedForGood` does (Release).
- **Approvals.** A release before then is a `Release` Operation, and needs approval as the release of an Allocation that its retention policy keeps (HBEP-10, What needs approval; open question 8). Draining an outranked peer is that table's "Drain an Instance while another stays ready", which needs none while its Allocation is kept until its hard end; under `ReleaseWhenIdle` it is the controller's own release under the spec, with no Operation. No row is added.
- **A pending extra is never cancelled for ranking.** A cancelled job's queue age is lost (open question 1, Why). It costs no grant hours, holds one slot within k's bound, and is ranked when it starts and passes. Shutdown, deletion and Operations cancel it as any pending Allocation (Release).
- **A gate failure is different.** It is a failed check, and its Allocation is released at once (above). The owner's concern is for surplus Allocations that passed, not for broken nodes.

**Hedging's started losers** (Hedging, Started losers are kept: "once question 12's ranking is built, a started loser is instead an over-allocation candidate"). With `overAllocation` 1 and no surplus or warm Allocation of the deployment on the loser's Provider, a member whose job has started when the winner serves is not ended at the win. It is that Provider's surplus Instance, runs its checks and reaches its ranking point. It is ranked as soon as it passes, since the winner is `ready`, against the hedge's winner alone, by time per output token, whatever their classes, and never against the deployment's other primaries on its Provider (revised by the Fable review of PR #612). Faster than the winner by more than the 10% margin, it becomes `ready` and serves in the winner's place: the winner drains if it serves, ends with `RankedOut`, and is kept as a warm spare under its policy. Otherwise it ends with `HedgeLost`, as Hedging says (PR #602), as a qualified warm spare. A winner that has a live replacement is in its handover, which ranking leaves alone, so the loser then ends with `HedgeLost`. One that fails a check fails like any Instance, and its Allocation is released, since a broken node is no spare (proposal). With `overAllocation` 0, or a surplus or warm Allocation already there, it ends at the win, as Hedging says.

**The cost in grant hours** (proposal; the owner kept k = 1 as the default at this cost, open question 12). Each round's extra costs its GPUs from its job's start until it is ranked in, or, ranked out, until its policy releases it. On Athena `maxIdle` bounds that to about the class's start-to-ready time plus 30 min. On a platform with no `maxIdle`, under `Keep`, a ranked-out Allocation holds its GPUs to its hard end, which for a one-Instance deployment about doubles the round's GPU-hours. A pending extra costs none. An extra ranked in costs nothing more: the peer it outranked costs the same instead.

**How k adjusts** (open question 12: "adjust it from the measured qualification failure rate"; proposal for the measure).
- **Recorded.** Each start-up outcome (Hedging, The width, `startOutcomes`) also keeps the gate's verdict when its Instance reached the gate: `Passed`, `Missed`, `Suspended`, or `RecordOnly` with no entry or an aged one (proposed), with the entry's `date`. It is an amendment to HBEP-11's API, as `startOutcomes` is. An Instance ranked out before `ready` adds a success, since it passed every check.
- **The rate.** A class's qualification failure rate is its outcomes that failed in `verifying` (a rehash `VerificationFailed`, or `QualificationFailed`: a failed check, the gate, or a warm-up that didn't settle) over its outcomes, shown as "failures of count" from the first outcome and unknown only with none, as the start-up rate is. A platform's pools its partitions' classes, as the start-up rate does.
- **Used.** Beta shows the rate with its count, beside the start-up rate, and k stays `overAllocation`. No rule for adjusting k is decided. Lean: k = ⌈N × f ÷ (1 − f)⌉ on a Provider with N primaries and rate f, so that N + k Allocations are expected to give N that pass.

**Events and conditions.**
- A ranked-out Instance raises `replaced` with reason `RankedOut` (proposed), regarding it, with the Instance it ranked below, or that outranked it, in `related`.
- A suspended entry raises `degraded` with reason `ReferenceSuspect` (proposed), regarding the ModelDeployment, naming the entry.
- Both join HBEP-8's Reasons table with the code that raises them, since the table is checked against the code. A gate failure is the existing `QualificationFailed`. No `Degraded` reason is added, so the ranking of `Degraded` reasons is unchanged.
- The Instance's `Qualified` message names the gate's verdict: passed, with the measurements and the limits; or recorded only, and why (no entry, the entry's age, or a suspended entry).

**Prior art.** Farley et al.'s placement gaming and Ou et al.'s keep-good, discard-bad (Notes) are the precedents. New here: a reference keyed on the deployment ID and measured by the gate's own method, the gate's retry and suspension, the serving peer's 10% margin, and the ranked-out Allocation kept as warm capacity.

### Release

Replacement before release (HBEP-3) applies only while the deployment keeps its desired capacity:
- Handover, and replacing a failing instance, while desired instances are above zero: the old instance is released only after its replacement serves. An Allocation whose Instance failed serves nothing, so it is released at once.
- Scale-down: the surplus instances drain and end, without replacement.
- Shutdown at the deployment's end time: every instance drains and ends, and pending Allocations are cancelled, without replacement.
- Deletion of the ModelDeployment: as shutdown, then the objects are removed (HBEP-11).
- Approved early termination, an `End` Operation (HBEP-10): as shutdown, before the end time.
- Repurposing: as scale-down for the old deployment, which is ending or scaling down. The Allocation then hosts the new deployment's Instance.
- Hedging (beta): when a hedge's winner serves, its pending losers are cancelled with no approval (open question 8), and a started loser's Instance ends while its Allocation follows its retention policy. A loser that is already `ready` drains first, as a surplus instance does (Hedging).
- Over-allocation (beta): a ranked-out Instance drains first if it serves, and ends with `RankedOut`; its Allocation follows its retention policy. Ranking never releases one (Over-allocation and qualification (beta)).
- In every case, an Allocation left with no Instance follows its retention policy (Retention), except one with `RelayEndedForGood`, and one whose ModelDeployment is being deleted, which are released at once. A user who deletes a deployment releases its GPUs, whatever its policy: `ReleaseWhenIdle`'s idle limit, like `Keep`'s, is for a deployment that still exists.
- A lease end is not a release: under the drain rule, an instance whose replacement is late drains and ends with its lease.

Releasing an Allocation means deleting it. Its finalizer cancels every job with its tag (HBEP-4, HBEP-11).

**The release cause** (beta; HBEP-10, Audit). Before each delete of an Allocation, the controller writes the annotation `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/release-cause` on it (HBEP-11): the rule followed, the reason it already computes for the delete (`internal/lifecycle/writes.go:106`), or the Operation's namespace, name and UID. The delete carries a UID precondition (`internal/lifecycle/writes.go:111`). So the audit log holds the cause before the delete, and a retention release names its policy with no approval asked.

### Retention

An Allocation that hosts no Instance follows its retention policy, unless it has `RelayEndedForGood` or its ModelDeployment is being deleted (Release). That one is released at once whatever its policy, since nothing placed on it can be reached (HBEP-6, What ends a relay; open question 8):
- keep it, as warm capacity, until a date, a grant's end or a deadline;
- or release it when idle, after an idle limit.

The default is keep until the deployment's end time, or until the grant's end or the deadline when one is set (open question 13). A kept Allocation still ends at its hard end. Where the profile sets `maxIdle`, an empty Allocation is released after that long even under keep. Its agent idles meanwhile. Placing Instances on warm capacity follows open question 14.

**The idle anchor** (science-agent decision, 31 Oct 2026). Retention's `idleLimit` and the profile's `maxIdle` count from the Allocation's annotation `hpcburst.groundnuty.github.io/idle-since`, which only the lifecycle controller writes, with a resourceVersion precondition:
- when the Allocation's last Instance ends, it is set to the latest `status.endTime` of the Allocation's Instances;
- when the job starts with no Instance placed on it, it is set to the job's start (`status.atProvider.startTime`);
- when an Instance is placed on the Allocation, it is removed.

An Allocation is idle exactly while it carries the annotation. The anchor lives on the Allocation, not on an Instance, because Instances can be deleted while their Allocation is kept, and it survives a controller restart.

### Repurposing

An operator, often an agent that needs throughput across several models, may move an Allocation to another ModelDeployment when a model's work finishes. The controller then:
1. drains the old Instance;
2. starts an Instance of the new deployment in the same Allocation;
3. verifies and warms it up before it takes traffic.

The Allocation keeps its name, and the new Instance has the new deployment's ID. Conversions between models are this operation. Fit and cost: open question 7. It is asked for by an Operation with action `Repurpose`: planned, its cost shown and confirmed like any other, with no approval (owner, 14 Nov 2026; HBEP-10, Repurposes). The controller refuses one that fails question 7's rule. Not in alpha: an alpha Allocation stays with the ModelDeployment that created it.

### Events and conditions

- **Conditions are the level truth** (HBEP-11). Events are best-effort Kubernetes Events (`events.k8s.io/v1`) in alpha. Their `action` carries HBEP-8's type and their `reason` the reason code alone; their `type` is `Warning` for `degraded` and `failing`, and `Normal` otherwise (HBEP-8, Events).
- **On a ModelDeployment:**
  - `Ready`: ready Instances at least as many as desired, with reason `EnoughReady` when true and `NotEnoughReady` when false. For a managed source, the catalog entry is listed and its Provider's key is not rejected, with reason `CatalogEntryUnlisted`, `CredentialsRejected` or `ProviderNotOnPlatform` when false (Reconcile loop, step 1);
  - `Degraded`, true with one of these reasons, and false with `AsDesired`:
    - `ReplacementLate`, `RestartLoop`, `VerificationFailed` and `SubmissionRejected`;
    - `CapacityDropped`: fewer ready Instances than desired, once the deployment has been ready, that is, with a recorded start-to-ready time or an Instance with a ready time. A deployment that has never been ready is not degraded for it (`internal/lifecycle/status.go`);
    - `ProviderUnavailable`: an allowed Provider that is not managed can't be used, even while another serves. It is missing or not `Ready` (Reconcile loop, step 1), its PlatformProfile is missing or invalid or not a batch profile, it names a `qos` the profile doesn't list, it allows no partition or one its profile does not describe, a partition it would use has no agent build, or the profile can't size the partition: no per-GPU values (`gpusPerNode` or the QOS's `maxGpusPerUser`, `cpusPerGpu`, `memPerGpu`), no time limit at all, a `maxTime` that doesn't parse, or a QOS cap that leaves no CPU or memory (`internal/lifecycle/topology.go` and `provision.go`). Also, when a needed Instance can't be placed and no other reason explains it, as with a selection that matches no batch Provider, no allowed Provider can take a new Allocation (`handover.go`);
    - `SpecUnsupported`: a `data-parallel-size` above 1, a workload shape that does not fit the declared context limit, a `generation-config` other than `auto`, an `override-generation-config` or an `hf-config-path` (The allocation agent, What it runs), an engine flag the launch sets itself, a parallel size, `max-num-seqs` or `max-model-len` that is not a positive integer, a missing `workload` or a workload value out of range, a `leaseLimit` that does not parse, a GPU count that no usable allowed Provider can host at first placement, counting only the Providers that are not too short, or a pending Instance whose own Provider can no longer host its GPU count (`internal/lifecycle/spec.go`, `topology.go`, `handover.go` and `provision.go`). The operator edits the spec; for a GPU count, making a fitting Provider usable also clears it;
    - `FrontNotConfigured`: no front address and dial-home mode are configured, so no Allocation is submitted (Provisioning; HBEP-11);
    - `IdentityUnresolved`: a class's deployment ID cannot be computed, from a permanent Hub failure or a display-form collision, with HBEP-3's causes and retry rules. The operator fixes the token or the revision, or investigates the colliding record (Reconcile loop, step 2; HBEP-3);
    - `SubmitLimitReached`: a submission was refused for a full submit limit, told by Slurm's reason name, or no allowed Provider has a free slot for a missing primary. It names the Provider, the limit and Slurm's reason line (Provisioning; HBEP-4, QOS limits);
    - `NoOverlapSlot`: in no-overlap mode, from the primary's drain start until its successor is `ready`. It names the Provider and the limit (Handover);
    - `PartitionTooShort`: an allowed Provider's time limit is not longer than the lead an Instance needs. It names the partition, the time limit, the bound that set it and the lead (Provisioning).

    These three are condition reasons only. The gap's event is the drain start's `CapacityDropped` (HBEP-8), so HBEP-8's event reasons are unchanged.

    **The ranking** (science-agent decision, 1 Nov 2026, from PR #151's review). When several reasons apply, the condition carries the first in this order, and its message lists every one that applies, as `degradedOrder` on `main` does (`internal/lifecycle/status.go`): `SpecUnsupported`, `PartitionTooShort`, `FrontNotConfigured`, `VerificationFailed`, `IdentityUnresolved`, `RestartLoop`, `SubmissionRejected`, `NoOverlapSlot`, `SubmitLimitReached`, `ProviderUnavailable`, `ReplacementLate`, `CapacityDropped`. `main`'s order holds all twelve, and #239's drift guard, in PR #246, is to check this list against it. So during a no-overlap gap the condition carries `NoOverlapSlot`, and a refused successor adds `SubmitLimitReached` to the message;
  - `Progressing`: true with reason `Provisioning` while the controller creates, starts or replaces Allocations and Instances, and false with `Stable`; false with reason `ManagedSource` for a managed source, which has no Instances by design.
- **On an Instance:** `Verified`, `Qualified`, `WarmedUp`, `Healthy`, `AgentUnreachable` and `TunnelConnected` (written from frps's proxy list; HBEP-6, Path state; the name is on `main`, in `api/v1alpha1/conditions.go`). In alpha a qualification records in `Qualified`'s message that no throughput reference exists. In beta the message names the gate's verdict: passed, with the measurements and the limits, or recorded only, and why (Over-allocation and qualification (beta)).
- **Events:**
  - `started`, `ready`, `degraded`, `failing` and `replaced`, as their HBEP-8 types;
  - `ending-soon`, when a ready Instance's drain start is less than an hour away and no replacement is ready;
  - `failing` with reason `HeartbeatMissed` when an Instance's `AgentUnreachable` turns True, when a Provider's last successful read is older than `ProviderReadyStaleness` (or, for a Provider never read, its `creationTimestamp` is), and when the front's `Lease` is stale. An absent front `Lease` raises none. It fires when the heartbeat goes stale and then at most once an hour while it stays stale (HBEP-8, Observers and Repeating conditions). A stopped Provider controller cannot write its `Ready`, so staleness is judged by age (`ProviderReady`);
  - `degraded` with reason `ObservationGap` once at start, regarding the `lifecycle` `Lease` (HBEP-8, Gaps after a restart); none with `--leader-elect=false`, which holds no `Lease`;
  - in beta, `replaced` with reason `HedgeLost` (proposed) for each hedge loser. It joins HBEP-8's Reasons table with its code (Hedging, Events and conditions);
  - in beta, `replaced` with reason `RankedOut` (proposed) for each ranked-out Instance, and `degraded` with reason `ReferenceSuspect` (proposed) for a suspended expected-performance entry. Both join HBEP-8's Reasons table with their code (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), Events and conditions).
- **Tests of events** check for at least one event of a type and reason on a resource, since client-go's recorder merges isomorphic events about an unchanged object and keeps only the first note (HBEP-8, What the recorder merges and drops). A message is checked on the condition.

- The reply to a submission is lost: the attempt is Unknown, never Failed, the controller resolves it by tag, and exactly one job with the tag has its hold lifted.

- Report order:
  - the agent's last report is delivered, then an older periodic report of the same instance: the controller keeps the last one, answers the older one with 204, and drops it;

### Compatibility and upgrades

- Stored state is custom resources, so the schema follows Kubernetes versioning (HBEP-11). The fields this HBEP adds are at `v1alpha1`.
- A controller upgrade must not touch running allocations. The new controller re-reads everything. Each Instance keeps its immutable `launch`, and each Allocation keeps its agent digest.
- The agent's protocol is versioned in its path (`/agent/v1/`). Within v1, a newer endpoint serves older agents, and fields an older agent does not know are ignored. New Allocations get the agent build the controller is configured with.
- **Report order** (#221). An agent staged before the upgrade that adds `seq` sends unnumbered reports. They are kept in arrival order, as before, so it keeps working until its Allocation ends.
- A downgrade restores the store (HBEP-11). A controller ignores status fields it does not know.
- **Over-allocation** (beta). A controller upgrade that adds it creates no extra for a running deployment until its next round, and a profile without `expectedPerformance` gates nothing, as before.
- **The queued-successor mark** (#195). A queued successor created before the upgrade that adds the mark carries none, so if it is still pending at the upgrade it records one cap wait. While fewer than 20 waits are kept, that one wait can set the planning statistic, so the upgrade is made with no queued successor pending. No queued-path run has happened yet, so no inflated sample exists on any real Provider, and #195's code lands before the first queued-path run, Helios's no-overlap run (beta) included. Alpha's one-slot run on `plgrid-now` creates its successor only after the slot frees, so the successor is unmarked and records a real wait; #195's mark gets no live run in alpha.

### Security

- Provider credentials belong to the provider plugins (HBEP-4).
- An Allocation's credential is generated by the controller, kept in a mode-600 file on the control-plane host, and placed on the platform in a mode-600 file (HBEP-4). It authenticates the agent's registration, which must also name the recorded job ID (HBEP-6). The Instance's key and a Hub token travel only over that authenticated channel, never in a file outside the job or on a command line. A Hub token is never written to a file on the platform: it stays in the agent's memory, or reaches a child process that downloads only through its environment (The allocation agent, Keys). On the control-plane host it is a mode-600 credentials file (HBEP-11, Credentials; HBEP-3).
- The controller sees no prompts. Warm-up and qualification use synthetic prompts only.
- Who may change desired state, ask for a repurpose, or approve a release, is HBEP-10's and HBEP-11's.

## Operational readiness

1. **Working:** reconcile age per deployment, and ready instances against desired ones. End to end, each deployment answers a synthetic request through the front. Ready below desired past `health.startTimeout`, `ReplacementLate`, or agents that stop reporting say it is not working.
2. **Restart:** it re-reads ModelDeployments, lists Allocations by label and Instances by owner, and reads the agents' next reports. Then it resumes planned handovers, and applies each empty Allocation's retention policy. Agents keep their Instances running while it is down; their reports resume when it returns.
3. **Silent failure:** "planned" handovers that were never submitted; Instances marked ready that serve nothing; a verification that read nothing. The lateness event, the front's synthetic requests, and the required settings in verification show the difference.
4. **Provider limits:** hard time limits, unknown start times, per-user QOS limits (planned within; a refusal is `SubmitLimitReached`, and a limit that leaves no overlap is no-overlap mode), fixed accounts, and center response times.
5. **Capacity:** no provider starts a replacement. The deployment degrades, and the question goes to agents (open question 10).
6. **Idle cost:** pending replacements cost no grant hours, but they hold queue slots under per-user limits. Overlap costs the hours of both Allocations while both run. On a short queue the replacement is planned from observed waits, so the two serve together for about the margin (open question 2), except while the Provider's kept waits hold a long early wait or a pend behind the user's cap (Handover, No-overlap mode, the cost). Before any wait is observed, or on a long queue, the replacement is submitted at the predecessor's start and can overlap it for most of the lease. In no-overlap mode there is no overlap and no cost for it; the cost is the gap in service instead. Over-allocation costs each round's extra from its job's start until it is ranked in, or, ranked out, until its retention policy, or `maxIdle`, releases it; a pending extra costs no grant hours but holds a slot (Over-allocation and qualification (beta), The cost in grant hours). A hedge's pending members cost no grant hours but hold a slot on each Provider; a started loser costs its Allocation's hours until its retention policy, or `maxIdle`, releases it (Hedging). Warm capacity costs each kept Allocation's hours until its policy, or `maxIdle`, releases it. Each agent holds one connection to the control-plane host.
7. **Rollback:** scale the deployment to zero. Instances drain and end without replacement (Release); pending Allocations are cancelled, and started ones follow their retention policy, which an operator can set to release when idle.

## Drawbacks

- Hedging, over-allocation, overlap and warm capacity spend queue slots and grant hours to buy continuity and throughput.
- The controller depends on each provider's queue semantics, which differ and change.
- The allocation agent is a second component to build, stage and keep compatible with the controller.

## Alternatives

- **Hand-run handovers by operators or agents.** This was the starting point, and it led to most of the problems in Motivation.
- **Slurm dependencies alone.** Same-node pins pended for days, dependencies counted from the wrong moment, and a cancel started dependents.
- **Keep whatever starts.** Simpler than over-allocation, but a broken or slow node then serves until someone notices.
- **Models in pods on Slurm (interLink and other virtual-kubelet offloading, Slinky's slurm-bridge).** Pods hide lease ends and start estimates, which the controller needs. The evaluation (28 Oct 2026) confirmed this; parts may become components.
- **Restarts through `srun --overlap` from the login node.** A step launched that way dies with the login-node `srun` (L-3.5), and keeping it alive is a long-running process on the login node (HBEP-4, open question 14).
- **Control files on the shared filesystem,** as www-2027's holder scripts used. They need no new channel, but every command and report costs a login-node command, and latency is the poll interval. The agent pulls its desired state over the dial-home path instead.

## Prior art and reuse

The controller applies by policy what www-2027 operators did per job: holder batch scripts that restarted on a control file, follow-ons that published a next endpoint for clients to switch to, a hand-run hedge that cancelled the loser only after the winner passed identity and timing checks, verified and warmed spares, and real-shape warm-ups. The allocation agent replaces the holder script.

- **2026-11-16: over-allocation and qualification, for beta** (User stories; Notes; Risks; Open questions 1 and 12; Controllers; What the controller reads; Reconcile loop, steps 6, 7 and 8; Instance start; Hedging; Over-allocation and qualification (beta); Release; Events and conditions; Test plan; Compatibility and upgrades; Graduation criteria; Operational readiness 6; Prior art and reuse). An amendment, the science agent's design, within the owner's decisions on open questions 8, 12, 13 and 14 and on the reserved slot and the warm spare of 15 Nov 2026, with HBEP-4's amendment of the same day for the profile's side. `overAllocation`, `RankedOut`, `ReferenceSuspect`, `expectedPerformance`, `suspendedReferences` and the outcomes' gate verdict are proposed. Revised by its Fable review (PR #612).
  - **The gate.** An Instance's throughput at the target sessions, per worker, is judged against the profile's matching `expectedPerformance` entry, keyed on the deployment ID, the capacity flags and the measured shape, with a tolerance of 100% by default and a 100 ms floor on time to first token. A miss is measured again after a rung-1 restart, and a second miss is `QualificationFailed`, whose Allocation is released as for any failed check. With no entry, an entry 90 days old, or one that two Allocations in a row failed, which status holds as suspended until its date changes, the gate records only.
  - **k.** `overAllocation`, 1 by default, adds one extra on each Provider where a pass creates new Allocations for missing primaries, within a bound of one surplus per Provider, with a slot beyond the reservations its own deployment's primaries will hold, and within the per-user caps. In host-dialed mode a deployment that hands over gets none.
  - **Counting and ranking.** The primaries are the `spec.instances` most advanced Instances, a `ready` one above one that has only passed; a `ready` surplus drains, and up to k surplus per Provider that isn't `ready` is left. A surplus Instance becomes `ready` only by outranking: it is ranked against its Provider's ready primaries with no live replacement, and outranks the worst only by more than 10% in time per output token. The ranked-out Instance ends with `RankedOut`, no failure, and its Allocation follows its retention policy. A replacement never waits for a ranking, so where one is due at its primary's start the round ends with no ranking, a stated limit.
